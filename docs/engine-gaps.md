# What the engine cannot say

Ten new genres were written in parallel, one author each, against the `Genre` and `Style` contracts as they stood. Each author was asked to report what they wanted to express and could not. This is that list, merged and de-duplicated, with the evidence each one came with — **and with what has since been done about it**.

It is worth reading as a compliment to the abstraction as much as a critique of it. Nothing here was a genre that could not be built; the seam held, and `generate/` and `render/` needed no changes to absorb arabic, indian, classical and metal. What follows is the set of places where an author had to write a comment explaining a compromise instead of a table expressing an intention — and the useful property of that list is that **most entries were found independently by two or more genres**, which is the difference between a gap and a taste.

## Where it stands

Twenty of the original twenty-three entries are closed, and six new ones have arrived since — pop, rnb and hiphop each brought some, and four of those six are closed too. §6 records what was actually built, because in four cases the fix has a different shape from the one this document proposed and the difference is the interesting part.

**All nineteen genres now exist**, holding 389 styles across 72 eras. `dnb` and `house` were the last two, and they were written against a §1 with nothing left in it — the first genres in the project for which that was true.

`npm run typecheck`, `npm run genres`, `npm run check` and `npm run concert` are all green as of this writing. Four staging failures that stood here for a day — a polysynth in 1952, a hand asked for nineteen semitones, a cable through a player, a one-armed hand drummer — are all in §6.

**`npm run check` is in that list for a reason worth one line.** The bass glide is the project's first control grid whose values can be *negative*, and `check-notation.ts` validated grid tokens with `/^\d+(\.\d+)?$/` — so the day a style first wrote a downward glide, 644 perfectly good bars were reported as unparseable notation by the check whose whole job is to be believed. That is the third time that file has held a vocabulary narrower than the one the renderer emits, and its header now says so. The fix keeps the signal rather than allowing minus signs everywhere: a negative is music in `penv` and a fault in every other grid, because a gain or a filter frequency below zero is a bug that would otherwise render as silence.

**It has since happened twice more, in one pass, and the count is now five.** The nudge grid is signed for a blunter reason than `penv` — `slotOf` rounds, so half of every correction is negative — and the trill writes the first slot token in the project with a **space inside it**, which tore `[c4 d4]` into two unrecognised halves because slots had always been split on whitespace. 320 correct bars, reported as broken. The splitter is depth-counted now. Five occurrences of one failure is no longer a series of accidents: **this file re-derives the renderer's grammar instead of sharing it**, and every table in it that answers *what belongs in a line* independently will go stale again.

**A second axis this list did not originally have.** Everything above is what a *genre author* could not say. There was a parallel set of things a **stage** could not say, and it was worse, because it had no authors to report it: the rooms and the clothes were written by people who could not see the result. That work is in §6, under *What a stage could not say*, and its lesson is one line — a bench is worth more than an assertion, because an assertion only catches what you thought to check.

**A third axis, and it is the one this list was structurally unable to see.** Everything above is something somebody tried to say and could not. §3.18a is something the engine *did* say, correctly, which the renderer then discarded on the way to the speaker — swing computed at 0.665 and played at 0.750, on every swung note in the catalogue, with `npm run genres` green throughout because it was reading `meta.swing` and not the output. An author cannot report that: from inside the genre tables everything looks right. It was found by asking a question from outside — *can the fiddle play a grace note?* — and following the answer down through two renderers. The lesson is the one §6's stage work already reached from the other direction: **a check that reads the intention is not a check.** Four of the six things fixed in this pass were found by parsing emitted output, and none of them by reading a table.

**A caveat about how this list was made, which four entries have now earned.** These are authors' reports, and an author who could not find a mechanism reasonably concluded there was none. Twice that was wrong at the time of writing — ghost notes and per-section layer selection both existed in partial form, on `Feel` and on `Chart` — and twice more the *fix* revealed the framing was off: the hand drum did not want a `DrumSource` at all, and this document asserted a sightline check that did not exist. Where a partial mechanism exists it is now named, because the shape of a fix is usually *compose with the thing that is already there* rather than *add a second one beside it*.

## 1. Open, and blocking the work that is still queued

**This section is empty of blockers.** Three of the five unwritten genres have been written — pop, rnb and hiphop — and the two mechanisms `dnb` and `house` were waiting on are both built. What is left below is one real ceiling that everybody has learned to write around.

The three that landed brought six new entries with them, in §3. Two clear this document's own bar of having been found independently more than once.

### 1.1 Nothing ramps the tempo — **closed**, see §6

`bpm` was a range drawn once per song and used as a scalar everywhere after. `SongMeta.tempo` is a `TempoMap` now.

**Evidence, kept because it is what the shape was argued against.** Indian's qawwāli accelerates across its length — that is what the form *is*. Finnish folk has the same problem in the pelimanni repertoire. Both wrote it down as a compromise. A build in house or dnb is a tempo-and-density ramp and half of it was unavailable; a hip-hop record that drifts is a different thing from one that does not.

It was the largest item in this document by blast radius, and that was the whole difficulty: the tempo reaches the IR, both renderers and the concert clock, and the four consumers do not agree about what a tempo is.

### 1.2 Nothing drops out mid-section — **closed**, see §6

Both halves now. `Chart.exits` closed the section-shaped one; `Style.drops` and `generate/drop.ts` close this one. The entry is left here with its evidence intact because the evidence is what the shape was argued against.

`excludeLayers` is per-style and all-or-nothing, and `Chart`'s granularity is the section kind. There was no way to say *this layer stops for these four bars and comes back*.

**Evidence.** Reggae's dub is the canonical case: bass out for four bars, then everything back, is the single defining gesture of the idiom, and it was approximated with a `break` transition, a filter ramp and a mood's `restraint`. Funk's `minneapolis` wanted the same and settled for one-onset bass tables. A **drop** in house or dnb is this and the exit rule together — everything leaves, then everything returns.

**It is also the mechanism the layered-ambient goal needs.** The README already says the way to make music thin out under speech is to mute layers rather than lower a master bus, and the audition page's layer chips do exactly that at playback. What is missing is the *composition* being able to say so.

**Shape of the fix.** Genuinely a new axis; do not make `Chart`'s ordinal fractional. It is closer to `FeelSpan`, which already carries a `from`/`to` bar range. The agent that built `exits` left two things for it: everything downstream of `Section.activeLayers` has now been exercised against sections thinner than their neighbours and came through clean at a 24% rate, and **the melody guard is a ready-made safety rule** — a layer may only be removed where something else is guaranteed to sound. A drop will hit that wall much harder, because it removes layers for bars rather than for sections.

### 1.3 Bass riff span is capped at about twelve semitones — **closed**, and the apologies it left behind are cleared

The mechanism closed some time ago and nobody noticed, including this document. The entry keeps its evidence because the evidence is what the shape was argued against, and because *how* it closed is the lesson.

**The entry as it stood was quoting a comment, not the code.** It said `generateBass` "places the root within a tritone of MIDI 40 and repairs only by whole octaves" — which is the wording of funk's `octave-drop` comment, and which stopped being true when `SHAPE_CEILING` gave a shape its own window and `placeRoot` began scoring every octave of the root instead of stopping at the first that fitted. Both landed while this entry sat here describing the code they replaced.

**Evidence, from six genres independently** — three more than the entry credited. Funk reworked `octave-drop` to take the ♭7 above the root rather than below, and thirteen further funk figures copy that spelling with nothing recording why — this entry said eleven until the sweep counted them, because it had counted the ones carrying a comment. Reggae's `steppers-octave` wanted a fifth below the root, a span of 17, and was cut to 12. Metal's `fifths` reached a nineteenth and folded 8 bars per style across 11 styles before it was cut to 12. dnb and hiphop each carried a *file header* stating the ceiling is a twelfth; house restated it inside `303-sixteenths`. **228 of the catalogue's 1,034 bass figures sat at exactly 12** — 173 of them distinct shapes, the rest the same figure appearing in more than one style.

**Measured, and the number is arithmetic rather than taste.** The reach is `SHAPE_CEILING - BASS_RANGE[0]` = 35 semitones and the candidate placements are an octave apart, so a shape of span `S` may stand anywhere in a window `35 - S` wide, and a window 11 wide or more always contains a candidate. **Every span up to 24 — two octaves exactly — fits at all twelve roots.** Past it the failure arrives one root at a time: 25 folds at one root in twelve, 30 at six, 36 nowhere at all. The curve is identical for every lo/hi split the catalogue writes, because only the width enters the arithmetic. **Across all 1,034 bass figures in the nineteen genres, 497 of which carry a shape at all, nothing folds at any root.** The cost of this entry was never in the bars — it was entirely in the tables that were narrowed to avoid it.

**Latin is the case that proves the fault was the silence and not the wall.** Its tumbaos span 19 and `timba-line` spans 22 — the widest shape in the project — and every one of them is placed correctly. Latin is the only genre that wrote what it wanted, and the only one that never found out there was a limit, because its figures carry a `cycle` and *"a riff is the same shape over every chord quality"* skips a cycled figure. It was right by luck.

**What was done, and what was deliberately not.** No engine change: widening the window further would move every bass line in the catalogue to fix zero bars, and choosing the placement by the figure's own span is what `placeRoot` already does. What was missing is that nothing ever told an author the number. `unplaceableRoots` now exports the question the generator asks itself, and `npm run genres` asserts *"every bass figure has an octave it fits in"* over all 1,034 — total, because it reads `spanOf`, so a cycle, a chord function mixed among the numbers, a `tone: 'octave'` and a glide destination all count exactly as they count when the note is placed. Green at 0. The old check remains the stronger evidence where it applies and it applies to 287 of the 497 shaped figures; this one covers the 210 it cannot see, including all eight of the widest.

**The sweep is done, and it took three notes back.** It was a table job rather than a mechanism one, exactly as §8 predicted at the end of the sidechain wave: *a fixed limitation leaves its apology behind in the code, and every one of those is a future author writing a compromise nobody needs.*

- **Metal's `fifths` has the octave above again** — root, fifth below, fifth above, octave, which is the power chord with all three of its voices and is what a figure of that name was for. Span 17, on the eleven styles that fold it in.
- **Reggae's `steppers-octave` has the fifth below the root**, the note its comment named and gave up. Span 17.
- **Funk's `octave-drop` has its ♭7 back under the root**, where `the-one` two rows above it already puts it. Span 14.
- **The thirteen other funk figures spelling the ♭7 at 10 keep it**, re-read one at a time. Nine reach it as a step in a line that also touches the octave — seven coming down off it, two climbing to it — and four never touch the octave at all, so they span 10 apiece and nothing was ever traded. A spelling shared with a workaround is not itself a workaround, and that is the half of this sweep worth writing down.
- **dnb, hiphop and house moved nothing, deliberately.** A sub, an 808 and a 303 line are each one register wide; the gesture all three headers named — the octave under the drop — is twelve semitones from whichever end it is written, so the width was never what the wall cost them. What they lose by correcting the comment is the *reason* they gave for writing the octave upward, and that turns out to be a real one on its own: written up, `placeRoot` leaves the root at MIDI 29–40 with the answer above it; written as `-12`, the root climbs to 40–51 to make room underneath, which takes the sub off the bottom note.

**215 figures now sit at exactly 12, down from 228**, and every comment that names a ceiling names 24. The split is the point of the entry: three of the six genres wanted a note back and three wanted nothing but a true number — which is the same answer the wall was giving them, arrived at deliberately for the first time.

## 2. Open, and structural

### 2.1 `applyShot` and the fill vocabulary were kit-only — **closed**, see §6

All four sites, in two waves. The entry is left here with its evidence intact because the evidence is what the shape was argued against, and because the second wave is the better lesson of the two.

`applyShot` wrote `bd` + `sd` + `cr`, hard-coded. `generate/fills.ts` and `KitVariation` were the same. So a genre with no kit got kit strokes in the one place it could not refuse them.

**Measured**, by the agent that generalised the solo: after its change, kit strokes written inside a hand-table drummer's own solo blocks fell from 2054 to **9** across 120 songs per genre — and all nine survivors were 3 stray `oh` in arabic and 6 stray `cr` in latin, arriving from fills and shots rather than from the solo generator. That agent also wrote the check, `a hand-drum genre never sends for a kit`, confirmed it failed on those nine, and left it out rather than commit a red check. It went in with the fill and shot wave and has been green since; it is the check `npm run genres` now runs over 241 songs on tables with no kit in them.

A **tihai** is exactly a `shot`, and `shotFigures`' metre fallback already resolves to the vibhāg heads for free — so indian gets one the moment the delivery stops naming three instruments that music does not have. **Both genres have now adopted it**, and the numbers are in §6.

The adoption re-measured the claim rather than trusting this document, which was the right instinct twice over. `applyShot` is called `playShot` now; on a hand table its `shot` is `['lp']`, and across 3,246 forced shot bars in all 28 indian styles there are **0 kit strokes**, with a shot bar containing exactly `{lp}` and landing on sam 100% of the time. But two things the *comments* asserted were never true rather than newly false: the five indian styles that already had palettes were described as the rooms that own a kick, a snare and a crash, and **none of the five has a kit either**; and `staging.ts` argued a riser on the grounds that those numbers stage a session drummer, where the measurement is **0 of 300 indian numbers putting anybody on the riser**. That second one is a real loss correctly taken — the second man was being staged by a seam gesture conscripting an instrument nobody had cast.

**The fourth site is why this stayed open after the ninth stray was gone.** `KitVariation` never wrote a wrong stroke — `HAND_VOICES` was `rd sh hh oh` and a table of `lp mp hp tb` has none of the four in it, so `handOf` returned nothing at all. The symptom was silence, in all 54 hand tables, in every section of every song. A check counting wrong voices cannot see a gesture that never happened.

### 2.2 There is no archetype for a vocal group — **closed**, see §6

Built and now adopted. The entry keeps its evidence because the evidence is what the shape was argued against.

`ARCHETYPE_OF` staged every choir patch — `choirAahs`, `voiceOohs`, `synthChoir` — as a **synthesiser**, because there was nowhere else for them to go. That is right for 1985 and wrong for 1932.

**Evidence.** It cost country's string-band era its documented vocal-quartet pad when the pre-electronic rig anachronism was fixed: the era comment said *"the pad is people"*, and the only honest substitute available was a pump organ. It is also relevant to gospel, doo-wop, barbershop and the close-harmony styles country already writes.

It got an `Archetype` member, an `ArchetypeSpec`, its own model, and the year-gated remap — which is `EARLY_ARCHETYPE_OF`, reached only from `archetypeForTrack`, and the gate is **`CHOIRS_GET_A_KEYBOARD = 1970`**. That number is worth writing down because it has already been got wrong once inside the codebase: `vocal-group.ts` said 1963, which is `SYNTH_RIGS.polysynth.from`, and the two are exactly the conflation `EARLY_ARCHETYPE_OF`'s own doc argues against. A synthesiser existing is a different fact from a choir patch being the ordinary way to get the sound, and the gap between them holds country's nashville era — 1968, staging the Jordanaires on 43% of its pads under a comment saying it could not.

### 2.3 A drum solo is named after a kit whatever plays it — **closed**, in two waves, see §6

`Section.solo.instrument` hard-coded `'drum kit'`, so a darbuka chorus was announced as a kit on the showbill. Nothing broke — `playerFor` still found the right performer — but the programme lied, and it lied about the one number in the set where the object is the point.

Small, and it wanted the same `drumStations` question the solo generator and the check now both ask. **That closed half of it, and the entry stayed open for the wrong reason: it was recorded as untouched, when in fact the hand-drum half had already shipped.** Arabic prints `darbuka`, indian `mridangam`, latin `congas`. What nobody had asked was whether there is anybody playing at all — see §6, where the other half is, and the 22% it was wrong about.

## 3. Expressiveness, wanted by one or two genres each

### 3.1 `ending` and `countIn` are genre-level — **closed**, see §6

`Style.ending` and `Style.countIn` exist, both are overrides of the genre's answer, and all six styles that reported the gap have adopted. Classical's nocturne and impressionist prelude fade; finnfolk's `runolaulu`, `itkuvirsi`, `karjanhuuto` and `konserttikantele` fade and are not counted in.

The evidence is kept because it is what the shape was argued against. Classical wanted `button` for 24 styles and `fade` for two; it got a chord struck where the sound should stop. Finnish folk is the same shape with a bigger exception.

**One number in this entry was wrong and the source file was right.** This said finnfolk's archaic material is "roughly a quarter of its catalogue"; `finnfolk/index.ts` said *"about a sixth"* and named four styles. Both were describing something true and neither said which: **four of twenty-four styles is a sixth of the catalogue, and the weighted draw is 71 of 300 songs — 23.7%**. The genre file now carries both figures and the distinction between them.

**What the wrong ending cost was measured, and it is not the crash cymbal this document would have guessed.** All six styles carry `excludeLayers: ['drums', …]`, so `landEnding`'s crash-and-kick branch has never fired for any of them. See §6 for the pitched half, which is real.

### 3.2 `TitleContext` carries no key or mode — **closed**, see §6

`TitleContext` carries `tonic` and `mode` now, and all three genres that reported it have adopted: arabic names the maqam, indian the rāga, classical the key.

The evidence is kept for the same reason. Arabic's repertoire names its pieces *form plus maqam* — *Longa Nahawand* — and the maqam is a function of the key. Indian's are named for the rāga and the tāla. A large share of real classical titles name a key. All three fell back to imagery or to the form alone, because a randomly drawn maqam name is a wrong label rather than a poetic liberty.

**Two of the three refusals were answered by code that predated them**, which is this document's own recurring finding arriving inside an entry rather than beside one. See §6.

### 3.3 No per-section scale change — **closed, and half of it refused**

A maqam's *sayr* is its habitual path, and a long piece modulates to a neighbouring maqam and comes back. A rāga performance does something comparable. `scaleForChord` was asked per chord and had no section to key on.

**Re-measured before it was built, and genuinely open, which is worth recording because this document has been wrong about that five times.** `scaleForChord`'s only piece-invariant inputs were the tonic and the mode, both fixed for a song, so a genre whose scale is a function of the key returned one scale and could not return two: **arabic and indian reached exactly 1 distinct scale per song over 40 seeds each**, against jazz's 5, latin's 5 and classical's 6 — the engine was never rigid, the scale simply could not move *by section*. Both plausible workarounds were re-measured shut. Riding `Section.transpose` gives **0 transposed sections in 40 songs** of each genre, because `keyChangeChance` is 0 on all eight eras; and keying on a chord unique to one section fails because **0 of arabic's 21 styles and 0 of indian's 28** have a section whose chord vocabulary is disjoint from every other's — arabic's entire genre vocabulary is six symbols, `i iv VI VImaj7 isus4 iv7`.

**The design question was whether to fill `Section.mode` or add an argument, and they are genuinely different questions.** `Section.mode` has existed since long before this, written in two places, never anything but the song's own mode, and it looks exactly like a dead slot of the right shape. It is a live slot of the wrong one. `Mode` decides how the roman numerals are read, so moving it moves the **chords**: on arabic's canonical tonic of D, `major` is Hijaz and `minor` is Kurd, and the only pitch class that differs between those two maqamat is **the third** — the note in every tonic triad in the section. Arabic's header already rejects that by name. §3.3 wants the opposite: the scale moving while the tonic and the chords stand still. So the mechanism is a fourth argument, `section?: SectionKind`, and `Section.mode` now carries a comment saying it is not the seam, so the next author does not retry it.

**The parameter cost nothing, exactly as predicted.** Trailing and optional, so none of the 19 genre or 66 style implementations had to change; bound once at the top of the per-section loop as a three-argument closure, so `generate/solo.ts`, `tune/types.ts`, `tune/tune.ts` and `tune/adapt.ts` never learned it exists. Hashing MIDI and Strudel bodies for **all 389 styles at 5 seeds each — 1,945 songs across all nineteen genres — the parameter alone moved 0 of 1,945**.

**Arabic adopted and the sayr is real: 186 of 200 songs now change maqam mid-piece**, 817 of 2,043 sections departing — `verse` 545, `solo` 232, `bridge` 40. The 14 that do not are exactly the 14 taqsim draws. All five departures fire: Hijaz→Hijazkar 295, Kurd→Farahfaza 218, Nahawand→Farahfaza 129, Hijazkar→Hijaz 116, Farahfaza→Nahawand 59. **Every row moves exactly one degree**, because a modulation here is one *jins* swapped for its neighbour rather than a new key, and **every destination is a maqam the seat tables already produced** — the table closed on itself with no new `ScaleName` and no new name. Nahawand and Farahfaza were already documented as *the same maqam family split by what sits above the fifth*; the sayr is that sentence used as a verb. On the blast radius: **92 of 1,945 rows moved, all of them arabic, 20 of its 21 styles**.

**Two findings from the 13 arabic rows that did not move**, both worth more than the feature. Six changed maqam and rendered identically because **no note landed on the degree that moved** — across 200 seeds only 2,165 of 81,975 notes in a departing section sound it, and 175 of 186 songs sound it at all. Two more had **no ghusn to depart on**: the duration trim in `buildForm` removes `verse` and `solo` from a form that overruns, and on the slowest cycles it removes every one, leaving an all-mazhab song. That is **14 of 840**, concentrated in `samai` 8/40 and `taqsim` 6/40, it predates this entry, and a song that is all refrain correctly has nowhere to go.

**Indian refused, and so did arabic's own `taqsim`, for the same reason.** Indian's central claim is that there is one rāga for the whole piece and the line never leaves it — its `scaleForChord` deliberately does not consult the chord — so a section-level modulation is close to the opposite of what that genre is. What indian actually reported is narrower and lives in §4: **a rāga cannot be left mid-phrase, which is what a thumrī does**, and the style's own header says so. This mechanism does not serve it. A section argument moves the scale **at a section boundary**, and a thumrī's bend happens inside the line — the style declares no transition palette for exactly that reason, that "the arrivals that matter are inside the line rather than at the joins between sections." `taqsim` refuses on the identical argument arrived at independently: it is unmetred, nothing is counting, its sections are a rendering grid rather than musical joins, and a taqsim's sayr unfolds mid-phrase. **Indian moved 0 of 200 songs and 0 of 2,293 sections, deliberately.** Two genres agreeing on where a mechanism stops is the useful half of this entry: **a gesture that lives inside the line is not served by an argument that fires at a boundary.**

### 3.4 `Effects` has no bell filter

Only low-pass and high-pass. Metal's 800 Hz mid-scoop — arguably *the* production signature of 1988 — is unrepresentable, and it was written into `eras.ts` rather than faked.

**Re-measured, and this entry blamed the wrong thing.** Its closing line used to say that now `Style.effects` exists, the tier is the remaining half of the same want. It is not: **metal writes `Style.effects` on 0 of its 24 styles, correctly**, because a mid-scoop is a fact about 1986–92 and `eras.ts` is where a fact about a decade belongs. The tier was never the problem. (21 styles across 6 genres do write it — pop 9, hiphop 4, house 4, rnb 2, reggae 1, dnb 1.)

**Neither renderer can play a bell**, which is the real reason and is worth the entry more than the field is. Of the **370 controls `@strudel/core` registers, none matches `peak|bell|shelf|notch|eq`**; superdough only ever sets a filter type of `lowpass`, `highpass` or `bandpass`, plus one `notch` hard-wired inside the phaser at a fixed centre. A serial low-pass into a high-pass is an *intersection* and cannot cut a hole. MIDI is worse: `controllersFor` documents that CC74 and CC71 are GM2 and defined relative to the patch's own filter, so the render "only ever uses it to darken".

**There is one mechanism that would work, and it is already in the file for something else.** A scoop is a *parallel* low-pass plus high-pass summed, and `voiceParts` in `render/strudel.ts` already binds one note grid and emits it four times — a body plus three formant bands, each filtered, summed in the stack. Its own docstring argues the mirror case out loud: a vocal tract puts *peaks* on a full spectrum rather than deleting the troughs. A scoop is that machinery used subtractively, and it would be audition-only, joining the six fields already marked so.

**Left open with the finding attached rather than built.** The doubled part costs a second summed copy of every comp note, with a phase interaction in the crossover region, to express something the `.mid` must stay silent about — a worse trade than the duck's, which both renderers play to 0.075 dB. Metal's thrash-era comment is already an honest statement of the compromise.

### 3.5 `Genre.filter` moves per section; a wah moves per note — **closed**, see §6

Funk declared no `filter` profile at all rather than fake an envelope filter as a section sweep.

**The entry described the want correctly and the ceiling wrongly**, which is the failure mode §8 already names three times over: an author correctly reporting what they could not reach, and nobody afterwards re-reading whether it had become reachable. It reads as though the plumbing were the limit. It is not, and has not been for as long as `NoteEvent.brightness` has existed: that field is set per note in `generate/filter.ts`, `filterSweep` in `render/strudel.ts` emits it as a per-note `.lpf()` grid, and `brightnessSweep` in `render/midi.ts` emits it as a CC74 stream, one event per onset that moves. **What was section-shaped was the value's *source*, never its resolution** — `applyFilter` reads a `SectionPlacement` and a bar span, and nothing underneath it could say *this note*.

**Two reporters and a third genre that wrote the compromise**, which is this document's own bar and which nobody had counted. Funk is the author. House's `speedgarage` hit the same wall from the other side — its wobble is *"a filter moving under one held pitch"* — and this entry never counted it, because that complaint was filed inside a paragraph about the **bass glide**: one sentence in the style's header carried both halves, and it took the glide adoption to separate them. A reporter this document cannot see is a reporter filed under the wrong heading, not a reporter who stayed quiet. And **rock's `psych` is what the compromise looks like when a genre takes it**: `filter: { depth: 0.45, shape: 'ramp' }` with a comment reading *"a wah pedal rocked slowly open across a whole section is a 1967 gesture and there is no other way to write it down here."* Hiphop and dnb both name a wah in their own filter tables, but both are quoting funk rather than reporting, so neither is counted.

**The evidence the entry never had is inside funk's own tables.** `funkrock`'s comp carries a figure called **`wah-chank`**, and what it actually writes is eight eighth notes alternating `vel: 0.2` and `vel: 0.7`. That is the pedal spelled in *amplitude* — the one substitution `Style.filter`'s docstring explicitly refuses to make in the other direction — and it is a table somebody tuned by ear around a filter that could not move. `pfunk` says it twice in prose instead: *"a synthesiser has no fret hand and an envelope filter makes every note an event"*, once in the style header and once on the `sixteenth` bass figure, with nothing behind either sentence.

**What was built is `Effects.filterEnv`, and the shape of it is the interesting part**: it is not a new field beside `swell`, it is `swell` widened, because superdough gives a part exactly one filter envelope and two fields writing `lpenv` would have been a fault waiting to be found. The refusal that came with it — the rocked pedal, measured against the LFO that superdough actually has — is worth more than the adoption. Both are in §6.

### 3.6 `DrumPattern.cycle` is one number for the whole kit — **closed**, see §6

Djent is hands on the bar and feet on a seven. Metal carried the seven on the guitar and bass (`cycle: 7`) and had the kit state the grouping instead.

**The entry was verbatim correct and the cheapest fix in this document.** `DrumPattern.cycles` is a length per voice, `metal/djent` has adopted it, and the evidence is kept below because it is what the shape was argued against.

**One blocking line, and it was one line.** `generateDrums` builds a list of rows, one per voice, and walks it once — `ghosts` is a row in that list and so is every struck voice — and then handed every row the same `cycle: figure.cycle ?? slotsPerBar`. It is now `figure.cycles?.[voice] ?? figure.cycle ?? slotsPerBar`. Nothing else in the generator moved: the row list, the fill's clearing, `accentOf` and the per-hit jitter all reach a per-voice cycle because there is no other path for them to take. **This is `ghosts` and `rolls` a third time** — a `Partial<Record<DrumVoice, …>>` beside `voices`, same slot indices, carried through `varyPattern` with the hand — and the repetition is the argument rather than a smell: a `DrumPattern` is a set of rows, and every property a figure turns out to have is a property of a row. What made `cycle` a whole-kit number was never a musical claim, it was that the loop reading it predated the row list.

**The three interactions the read-only pass called clean are clean, re-verified.** `varyPattern` merges slots between voices, which across two cycle spaces would be nonsense — but a hand is only ever elected from `HandStation.keeps`, which is the cymbals and the brushes, and the kick and the snare are in neither, so feet on a seven never meets it. Fills already exempt the foot — `voice !== 'bd'` in the clear loop — and a drifting foot needs exactly that, since a figure that stopped for the fill would restart in the wrong phase and in a *different* wrong phase every section. And `accentOf` reads `slot % slotsPerBar` and stays right: a drifting kick takes the weight of wherever it lands.

The one case the pass did not have to consider, because nothing had adopted yet: a cycle written on a **hand** voice, which `varyPattern` could pour into a row already on another clock. The clock moves with the hand like the ghosts and the rolls do, and the merge is **declined** where the destination disagrees, which costs no random number because the plan has already been drawn.

**Measured, and the catalogue turned out to have been paying for this in nine places.** Of the 103 drum patterns carrying a `cycle`, **9 land in more than one place in the bar**; the other 94 are `cycle: 32`, a two-bar figure. **Seven of the nine are named after a single voice**, and what each had to do about it is the argument for the field: `dotted-hat` and `hat-against-four` write `hh` **and no other row at all**, so a section drawing one plays hats with no kick and no snare — `dotted-hat` is 6 of 18 in dnb's `halftime`, a third of its sections; `ride-drift` and `hat-against-five` are a ride and a hat with no drum in the pattern; and `walking-percussion`, `twelve-perc` and `twentyfour-perc` each drag a `bd` onto the percussion's clock. Only `drift-kit` and `drift-backbeat` say *kit* and mean it. Deleting the rest of the kit to drift one row of it is what a whole-kit `cycle` costs, in five genres, and none of the nine is touched here: they are tables somebody tuned by ear.

And the catalogue is **951 drum patterns, not the 1,005** this project has been repeating. 1,005 is exactly 951 plus the 54 hand tables. `parts.ts` and `docs/rhythm.md` both carried it; both are corrected, and the two numbers beside it — 640 patterns electing a hand, 54 hand tables — were re-measured and are right.

**The adoption**, and it is the smallest possible one. `grouping-kit`'s kick row was `bd: [0, 3, 7, 10, 14]`, which is `[0, 3]` stepped by seven and then **restarted at the barline**. It is now `bd: [0, 3]` with `cycles: { bd: 7 }`, renamed `seven-foot`, so bar one is unchanged note for note and bars two onward drift, coming home at bar seven where the guitar does. **37 of 37 kick onsets land on a guitar onset, against 35 of 40 before** — the guitar's seven and the kit's seven agree at every stroke, because both are phased from the top of the section and both step by seven. The five that used to miss were the barline reset. Over seven bars the kick uses all sixteen slots of the bar against five before, and its mean velocity falls 0.803 → 0.726, which is `accentOf` doing what it should: the foot is no longer pinned to a group head.

### 3.7 `metricStrength` always calls slot 0 the strongest — **withdrawn**

A masurkka's weight is on beat two. Finnish folk did it in velocities instead. Distinct from `groups`, which the same genre used successfully for the polska's uneven three — `[5, 3, 4]`, a 40:25:35 division matching the Nordic fiddle recordings to the nearest sixteenth.

**Literally true and withdrawn anyway, which is a distinction this document should make more often.** `rhythm.ts` returns 4 for slot 0 *before* it consults `groups`, and a group head only ever earns 3 — so the entry describes today's code exactly, at every metre, with or without a grouping. What it does not have is a second reporter.

Finnish folk, the one genre that filed it, has since withdrawn it in its own prose: *"`metricStrength` will still call slot 0 the strongest slot in the bar, because it is… that is enough, because a mazurka is recognised by what the band plays and not by where the barline is drawn."* And the obvious second reporter is not one — **reggae's one drop is the same musical shape**, accent on three with slot 0 deliberately empty, and it solves the whole thing in its tables without mentioning `metricStrength` once.

By this document's own bar — *found independently by two or more genres* — that makes it a taste rather than a gap. It also barely touches its own complainant: `masurkka` excludes drums, so `accentOf` never runs for it, and it declares `shots` explicitly, so the `metricStrength >= 3` fallback is bypassed too.

If anyone revives it, the honest framing is not *slot 0 is hard-coded* but *a style cannot say which slot is its strongest* — and it needs a second genre to ask.

### 3.8 No rubato

`Feel`'s `push` is a fixed millisecond offset per layer — a groove, not a phrase that stretches and gives the time back. Classical left `feels` absent rather than approximate it.

### 3.9 No "the same tune, decorated" — **closed**, and it never existed

`hook.recall` either replays a phrase or re-composes it. A da capo aria's ornamented repeat is neither.

**Except that it is, and had been for four days when this was filed.** `varyRecall` in `tune/tune.ts` is the third option, and its own docstring is this entry's title: *the same tune, with one thing changed.* It landed in `215c6f4`, **`Let a recalled chorus come back varied`, on 2026-07-30**; classical's `aria` header wrote *"there is no third option"* in `4229741` on 2026-08-03, and this entry copied that sentence. Every recalled tune goes through it and there is no code path that pastes a recall verbatim — so the style whose comment declared it impossible has been doing it since the day it was written. **Measured over 200 seeds: 300 of 363 recalled sections come back decorated, 83%.**

**This is the fifth entry to describe code that had already changed, and the worst of the five**, because the other four were at least describing a wall that had once existed. Here the mechanism was already running on the complaining genre's own output. §1.3 quoted funk's comment, §3.19 said compound time was unsayable while classical shipped it, §3.20 named a missing object that was on a bench — and all of them, including this one, were caught by reading source rather than by anybody noticing.

**What is genuinely short is the depth, and it is arithmetic.** `varyRecall` moves `max(1, round(amount × 2.4))` notes against an `amount` capped at 0.9, so it is one note or two and never three: **2.8% of the line at a typical draw and 5.0% at the cap**, where a baroque division redecorates every long note in the phrase. And the depth is not authorable — `amount` is a hard-coded expression in `generate/song.ts` that no `Style` field reaches, so `aria`'s `melody.ornament: 0.42`, the highest in its file and written expressly for this, does not touch it. Three operators exist (`liftPeak`, `ornamentLongest`, `holdLast`) and one of them changes only duration.

So the honest remaining want is *a style-side depth and a wider operator table*, which is a much smaller entry than the one it replaces and belongs to `src/tune/`.

### 3.10 `WORD_STYLES` has no sargam — **closed**, and the other half deliberately abandoned

An Indian vocal line should be sung on *sa re ga ma pa dha ni* or on a tarānā's *dir ta na dere*. `airy` was used, which reaches *ma*, *na*, *ni* and *re* and holds its vowels, but cannot say a stopped consonant. The indian author called a `sargam` entry the highest-value single addition for that genre, and was right — it binds syllables to scale degrees, which is the defect `airy` had.

**The tarānā half was built, measured and deleted, and that sequence is the useful part.** A `tarana` table existed for some time, was pointed at by nothing, and could not have been: a tarānā is a `Style`, `Genre.vocals` is one profile per genre, and no field on `Style` reaches a word style. So the first finding was that the entry had been written for a selector that never existed — this document's own recurring fault, one level down from a field nobody adopts.

The second finding is why it was deleted rather than plumbed. Swapped in behind indian's profile over **142,535 sung syllables**, its nine written onsets collapse onto **five sounded ones**: `LETTER_ONSET` puts `d` and `t` both on `stop`, and there is no aspirated or retroflex stop in `Consonant` at all — which are exactly the three distinctions a tabla's vocabulary is built on. The consonant-heavy table came out *less* differentiated than the vowel-led one it was written to contrast with, 5 distinct onsets against `sargam`'s 7, with `stop` alone taking 44% of every onset. Its own headline claim was the least true thing about it: it said its codas stop the vowel dead and offered `r m n`, a liquid and two nasals with no stop among them. And adopting it would have cost the `degrees` binding — correctly, since a bol names a stroke and not a note — returning those styles to a hashed lexicon whose syllables have nothing to do with their pitches, which is the defect `sargam` exists to remove.

What would reverse it is now written down in `indian/vocals.ts`: aspirated and retroflex members of `Consonant` in `core/types.ts` **first**, a per-style word style only after. Until then the entry was five sounds pretending to be nine.

### 3.11 The Japanese pentatonics still have no rows

The general problem — `makeScale` fixes degree 0 at the tonic, so a rotation needs its own row — is closed for the five scales that were blocking genres (§6). One set was deliberately left:

`koto`, `shamisen` and `dantranh` all have ranges and concert archetypes, and **no genre in the catalogue plays any of them**. Two reasons not to guess: hirajoshi is a *tuning* before it is a scale and there is no tuning concept (§3.12), and hirajoshi/iwato and kumoi/in-sen are two rotation *pairs* — so a Japanese genre will want four rows chosen together rather than one guessed in advance.

### 3.12 No instrument-tuning concept

Metal expressed drop tunings as register — `layerPlan.offsets.comp: -7`, low key weights — and wrote the limitation down.

### 3.13 `Style` is not a container

A Finnish *purpuri* is a wedding suite made of other dances. The hook setting and the form buy the feeling of a suite and none of its architecture.

### 3.14 `Attachments` exposes only `head` and `torso`

So a wrist accessory — studded wristbands, a watch, tape — has nowhere to anchor. Metal and the costume pass both stopped at this rather than thread a new anchor through `performer.ts` and `performer-arms.ts`.

### 3.15 Nothing subdivides below a sixteenth — **closed on both sides**, see §6

Built as `DrumEvent.roll` and `DrumPattern.rolls`, and adopted by `trap` and `drill`, the two styles the report named. **The melodic half is now built too**, as `NoteEvent.trill` / `NoteTrill`, and adopted by finnfolk — see §6 for why it had to be a declaration rather than an inference, and why the entry took a second pass to close.

The grid is sixteenths everywhere, and for eighteen genres that was never a limit worth writing down. A trap or drill hi-hat subdivides *inside* a stroke — triplets, 32nds, the roll that is the whole point of the part. **At 140 BPM a written sixteenth is 107 ms and the roll wants 36.**

Hiphop wrote what it could: the dotted-eighth chain and four consecutive sixteenths on the last beat. The faster rolls are simply absent, and there is no approximation that gets closer. Dnb filed the same entry from the other side and in the sharper words — *no stutter, no retrigger, no 32nd roll* — which makes this the only entry in the tail with more than one reporter and the only one either of them called a **technique** rather than an ornament.

Dnb is deliberately left unadopted; a second genre is a separate job now that the shape is proven.

### 3.16 A bass note cannot slide — **closed**, see §6

Built as `BassHit.glide`/`glideTime`, and now adopted by the genres that reported it.

`BassHit` was `at`, `dur`, `tone`. A drill 808 and a g-funk bass both *glide* between pitches, and both were written as two struck notes where the record has one that moves.

**Two styles hit this independently in one genre**, which by this document's own standard makes it a gap rather than a taste. It is also the nearest thing in the project to the pedal steel that country could not have (§3.12's neighbour) — the same missing idea, which is a pitch that is a function of time rather than a constant per note.

### 3.17 `Effects` has no envelope follower — **closed**, see §6

Built as `Effects.duck`, in decibels of gain reduction under the kick, and adopted by pop and house. It is not an envelope follower and did not need to be.

**The gate half is refused**, with its measurement, and is also in §6. Two separate things were found to make it unbuildable rather than merely awkward, and a third was found beside it: `DrumTrack.voiceEffects` — the "ready-made home" this entry named — is read by one renderer and **written by nothing**, in 0 of 380 songs across all nineteen genres.

### 3.18a The audition threw the swing away — **closed**, see §6

Not an author's report. Found by asking whether a fiddle could play a grace note, and discovering on the way that **the Strudel renderer was rounding every onset to a sixteenth and had always been**.

`applySwing` writes the offbeat eighth at beat fraction 0.665 and bakes it into `NoteEvent.beat`. `slotOf` rounds; `slotOf(0.665 × 4)` is slot 3, which is 0.750. So triplet swing was played as a dotted-eighth shuffle — measured on one medium-swing jazz song as **903 events, all of them, moved 33 ms late**, and 0.665 and 0.750 collapsing to the same token so the notation could not express the difference even in principle. Across nineteen genres **19 % of all onsets** were off the grid and being moved.

`npm run genres` was green throughout, and that is the part worth keeping. It asserts `meta.swing > 0.3` — a number on the metadata object, which was always correct. **Nothing was reading the output**, so a renderer could discard a fifth of the timing in the catalogue without a single check noticing.

Closed with a `.nudge()` grid rather than a finer resolution, which is also §3.15's answer arriving from the other direction: subdivision says *how many* strokes share a slot, nudge says *when* each one happens, and the two together are what a grid of fixed sixteenths could never say alone.

### 3.18 `swing` delays the eighth, and some idioms swing the sixteenth

`Style.swing` is documented as the shuffle between the two halves of a beat. New jack swing shuffles the **sixteenth**, which is a different subdivision and is the genre's name. Rnb wrote `0.16` as "the nearest honest object" and recorded that it is not the thing.

Adjacent, from the same author: **no per-stroke timing offset.** The `laidback` feel pushes a whole layer by one amount, but `offgrid`'s actual gesture is the second backbeat dragging *further* than the first. Approximated with slot displacement, which is audibly a different thing.

(This entry said `Feel.laidback`, which is not a thing. `laidback` is one of six `FeelId`s and the field doing the pushing is `Feel.push` — a small enough slip to look harmless, and precisely the kind that makes a future author search for a field that does not exist.)

### 3.19 Compound time cannot be stated — **closed**: it could, and had been for two days

The entry read: *Half the girl-group repertoire is 12/8, and `beatsPerBar` cannot say so without also claiming the bar is a 3/4. Pop approximated it as `swing: 0.28` over 4/4 and admitted the approximation; rnb's `doowop` did the same at `0.33`.*

It was a documentation gap, and this is the fourth in one stretch — after §1.3, §2.3 and §3.20 — so the pattern that entry names is now four deep.

**12/8 is `beatsPerBar: 6` at `beatUnit: 8` with `groups: [6, 6, 6, 6]`.** Twenty-four sixteenths in four dotted-quarter groups; `timeSignature` recovers the numerator by asking how many `beatUnit`s fit and writes a literal `12/8` into the MIDI header. Nothing was built and nothing needed to be: `metricStrength` already reads `groups`, already declines to find quarter-note beats inside a group that is not a whole number of quarters, and already puts strength 3 on each of the four dotted quarters. **`classical`'s `nocturne` and `lacrimosa` have been shipping in exactly this metre since `4229741` — two days before `a6c1d58` created `pop/styles.ts` and `rnb/styles.ts`.** Both authors wrote *cannot* about a thing already in the tree, and the shape they did find — `gospelsoul`'s 6/8 — is the same one at half the length.

**Why `swing` looked equivalent, and the one number that says it is not.** `applySwing` delays the second eighth of each beat by `swing / 2` quarters and touches nothing else. At `0.33` that lands the eighth 1.4 ms from a true compound third in `doowop` (539.2 ms against 540.5, at 74 quarters a minute) and 1.6 ms in `deepsoul` — so for a table that plays *two* notes to the beat the equivalence is real, and both authors were right about what they measured. The gap is the **middle** triplet, which no value of `swing` reaches because `swing` is not applied to the sixteenths at all: it wants 270.3 ms in `doowop` and 327.9 in `deepsoul`, and the nearest a sixteenth grid offers is 202.7 and 245.9 — **67.6 and 82.0 ms early**. A doo-wop piano plays all three. Both styles' comp tables therefore played a long-short pair, which is a shuffle, under a comment calling it a triplet.

**Both are converted.** `doowop` → `[96, 126]`, `deepsoul` → `[78, 105]`; `bpm` counts quarters and the felt pulse is the dotted quarter, so the ranges are the old ones times 1.5 and a bar lasts the same wall-clock it did. `triplet-piano` and `triplet-church-piano` play twelve strokes where they played eight, the ghosts move off the straight sixteenth onto the last eighth of the beat, and the files stop telling every notation program that opens them that a doo-wop ballad is in 4/4. Blast radius, over 1167 renders across all nineteen genres at three seeds a style: **the two converted styles, plus 19 of 60 `gospelsoul` titles** — see below.

**Pop's `girlgroup` is not converted, and that is the part with a measurement behind it.** Three reasons, all in the style's own header now:

- `0.28` is not a rounding of a triplet. At 127 quarters a minute it puts the eighth at 302.4 ms where a triplet wants 315.0 — **12.6 ms short**, a deliberate shuffle rather than a compound bar, chosen by an author who wrote 0.28 in a file where the neighbouring genre's author wrote 0.33.
- **7.9% of its onsets sit on a straight sixteenth** — 42.7% of the snare, which is `blaine`'s ghost row, and 9.2% of the comp. A straight sixteenth lands *exactly halfway* between two positions of a compound grid, 39.4 ms from either. Those notes do not shift under conversion, they have nowhere to go. `doowop`'s figure was **0.69%**, which is the whole difference between the two.
- The repertoire is genuinely two metres. The Blaine figure is a straight four with castanets on the eighths and it is the record the style is named for; the compound half is a different set of singles.

**So one thing this entry wanted is still missing, and it is not the one it named.** A `Style` holds one `beatsPerBar`, and a style whose repertoire is half compound and half straight has to pick. That is the same shape as the sama'i in §4 — `beatsPerBar` is song-level and some music changes metre inside itself — and it is worth more than a compound-time mechanism would have been, because the compound-time mechanism already exists.

**A fault found while fixing it, which is the fifth of those.** `rnb/titles.ts` struck the invitation families below 76 BPM and read `Style.bpm` to do it. `bpm` counts quarters, so a compound style reports half again too fast: `gospelsoul` says 70–96 and is counted at 47–64, and the gate had been calling a church 6/8 hymn fast enough to invite anybody to dance since the day it was written — **19 of 60 titles**. It now divides by `groups[0] / 4`, which is one felt beat in sixteenths, and the raw field is right for everything ungrouped.

### 3.20 There is no object for a person holding a microphone — **withdrawn**: both of its claims are false, measured

The entry read: *`Genre.vocals` casts the singer as a **doubling of an instrumental line**, so a rapper or a front singer stands in the row with the keyboard players rather than downstage of them. Distinct from §2.2, which wants a vocal group as an instrument; this wants the act.*

It is quoted rather than deleted, because it is the third entry in this one pass — after §1.3 and §2.3 — to have been describing code that had already changed, and three is a pattern rather than an accident.

**There is an object.** `ARCHETYPES.singer` — *family voice · 0 hands · stand · work height 1.55 m* — and `web/concert/instruments/singer.ts` builds it: a microphone, a stand and a cable, with the capsule racked to *this* singer's mouth rather than to the mean, because a 1.55 m capsule in front of a 1.92 m singer is a stand nobody adjusted. The hands are free on purpose and the argument is written twice, in the spec and in `gallery.ts`: free hands are most of what a singer's body is doing. It is item 32 on the `/models` bench and always has been.

**And the reason it is not in the prop vocabulary is written down too**, which is the part that should have stopped this entry being filed. `venue.ts`: *"Nothing else a player touches is a prop. **No microphone stand**, no music stands, no instruments: those belong to the instrument models, which know where the hands go."* Somebody had already considered a microphone as a stage object, decided which side of the seam it belonged on, and argued it against the harder case of a hundred music stands.

**Nobody stands in front of them.** `ROLE_OF.vocal` is `front`, `PROMINENCE.vocal` is `0` — the lowest, so the singer wins `frontCentre` outright — and `layoutFrontLine` has carried the rule verbatim for as long as it has existed: *when the number is sung there is a singer at a microphone front and centre, and the lead instrument moves back a step. Not sideways — back.* Half a metre plus, at `zDown - 0.7`.

**Measured, because the entry's own symptom is a measurable claim.** 360 sung songs cast across hiphop, pop and rnb, and then 131 sung numbers over the real `buildConcert` path in all nineteen genres. The singer is the `singer` archetype in 131 of 131, standing, never on a riser, at **exactly `lip − 0.950 m`** — the downstage limit — with a median |x| of **0.000**. Somebody downstage of them: **0 of 125** in the eighteen band-staged genres. Somebody even *level* with them: **0 of 125**.

**The six exceptions are ambient, and they are the picture rather than the fault.** `stageAmbient` is a different routine on purpose and says why: *there is no front line because there is no foreground — the genre's own rules say so, and the lighting honours it by having no follow spot at all… Downstage centre is excluded. It is the one position on a stage that means "look at me", and this music does not have one.* An ambient vocalist stands 3.9–5.9 m back among the installation, which is correct.

**What is left of the entry is a different entry, and it is not a staging one.** `LayerId.vocal` really is documented as *"sung line doubling the melody"*, and `generateVocalLine` really does syllabify the melody rather than compose against it. That is a fact about `generate/vocals.ts` and about what the voice *sings*; it has no consequence for where the singer stands, which is what this entry claimed and what it was filed under. Whoever wants it should open it as that, with a musical example attached — and should read the vocal aesthetic first, because the floaty vowel-led line is deliberate.

**Nothing was built for this and nothing should have been.** The whole cost of the entry was a list saying the stage could not do something it has been doing in every sung number in the catalogue — and, at the top of this document, a "tail" that was one item longer than it really was.

### 3.21 The audition strikes a drone once a bar, and every entry above was heard through it

**A new kind of entry, and the reason it is worth its own number.** Everything else in this document is something a genre author could not *say*. This is something the engine says correctly and **the audition discards on the way to the speaker** — so it is invisible from inside the tables, and it has been sitting under every by-ear judgement anyone has made about this catalogue.

`buildNoteGrid` cannot open a bar group with a sustain marker, because that is a parse error in mini-notation. So a note crossing a barline is **re-stated** at the barline instead of continued. Verified against emitted output rather than reasoned about: `ambient`/`drone` at seed `padproof` holds one pad chord for **64 beats** in the IR, and the audition emits

```
[[b2,d4,a4] _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _]
[[b2,d4,a4] _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _]
…
```

— **sixteen attacks, each with its own envelope, where the IR says one held drone.**

**Measured over 380 songs, by layer.** The audition changes the sounding length of **37.7% of all notes** (mean error 0.071 beats), because `end = slotOf(beat + duration)` rounds every duration to a sixteenth. Split by whether the instrument sustains, it is 49.7% against 22.6%. And the re-articulation:

| layer | notes | length changed | re-attacked at a barline |
|---|---|---|---|
| pad | 50,623 | **95.7%** | **50.5%** |
| melody | 84,374 | 90.3% | 8.5% |
| comp | 368,045 | 22.3% | 2.9% |
| bass | 152,497 | 19.4% | 2.0% |

Across 30 ambient songs the audition fires **1.34× more attacks than the IR has notes**.

**The layer the layered-ambient goal is built on is the layer the audition is least faithful to.** `.mid` has the exact lengths and always has, so this is a disagreement between renderers rather than a fault in the music — which is precisely why nobody found it: every check in the suite reads the IR or the emitted text, and both are correct.

**It cannot be fixed inside `render/strudel.ts`**, and that is the finding rather than a caveat. The constraint is mini-notation's, not this project's. It is the single strongest argument for the native player scoped in `docs/architecture.md`, and it comes with a free acceptance test: a 64-beat drone must sound **once**.

One accidental mercy worth recording before it is lost. Because the audition re-attacks the pad at every barline, the audition page's layer chips — which mute by re-rendering — currently land within a bar, 2.03 to 4.36 seconds in ambient. **Fixing the fidelity makes that strictly worse**, up to 64 beats, so the runtime gain path and the faithful player have to arrive together.

## 4. Small warts

- **`drumBanks` is required even for a genre with no percussion** — **closed**. Classical named `AkaiMPC60` in all four eras purely to satisfy the type. `generateSong` now falls back to `''` where the table is empty, which is a claim rather than a placeholder: `readBankName` splits it to a machine named nothing, `drumStations` puts nobody on the stand, and `resolveDrumSample` is never asked because there is no event. A new check in `genre-check.ts` holds the pairing the guard deliberately does not — *an era that names no drum bank has no style that plays one* — and it was proved by constructing the bad pairing, not by watching it pass.
- **A style with `excludeLayers: ['drums']` still needs a non-empty `drums` table** — **closed, and it is the same fix**. `generateSong` drew the figure before it knew about exclusions and an empty array threw in `rng.weightedBy`; measured at 0 of 20 songs both with and without the exclusion set, because the exclusion is built 150 lines earlier and was never read at the draw. The guard tests emptiness rather than exclusion, which is what keeps it free: `rng.weightedBy` costs one `next()` whatever it returns, so reading the exclusion there would have skipped a draw for all 54 placeholder styles and reshuffled every number behind them.

  **What this allowed deleting, and it is now deleted**: 54 of the 389 styles carried a `drums` table whose rows all had empty `voices` — 26 classical, 14 finnfolk, 7 country, 4 indian, 2 ambient, 1 arabic — every one of which also declares `excludeLayers: ['drums']`. Classical's `SILENT_BANK` in its `eras.ts` was the era-side twin, used by all four of its eras. **The population is 0 today**, `SILENT_BANK` is gone and all four classical eras carry `drumBanks: []`.

  **The deletion was not free, which is the part worth recording.** An absent table skips the draw, so the stream shifts and every decision downstream re-rolls: classical moved 215 of 220 rows, finnfolk 59 of 70. Nothing outside the six genres moved at all. It is a re-roll and not a fault, and that was checked field by field rather than asserted — style, era, mood, key, tempo and form come through identical in every song, because all of them are drawn upstream of the guarded line. What moved is note content and the occasional arrangement coin.

  **Indian and arabic emptied five tables between them and moved zero songs**, which is the most instructive number in the batch. The stream *does* shift — their very next draw, the bank, comes out different in 28 of 40 — and it reaches nothing, because a bank with no events is never emitted and because those five styles declare exactly one progression per section kind. **A shifted stream can only change a decision that had an alternative.** Country's and ambient's tables hold two to five, which is precisely why they moved.

  **The count was 54 and the edit was nearer six**, because most of it was shared constants rather than tables written out per style: finnfolk had one `SILENT` behind all fourteen, classical a `NO_KIT` behind all twenty-six plus `SILENT_BANK` on the era side, country a `NO_KIT` behind its seven, and indian a `NO_DRUMS` inside a `FREE` spread into all four. Every one is deleted. **This entry used to credit the trick to ambient**, and it is the one genre that never had a named constant — two inline literals, one of them commented. The attribution came from country's own comment saying it copied the idea from ambient, which was true about the idea and wrong about the name.
- **`keyChangeChance` couples two gestures** — **closed**. `EraProfile.bridgeKeyChangeChance` separates the bridge to IV or V from the final lift; absent means half of `keyChangeChance`, which is what every era got before and still gets. `rng.chance` consumes one `next()` whatever it returns and both draws in `planKeys` are unconditional, so an era declining the field generates bit-identical music rather than merely similar music — verified over 570 songs across all nineteen genres. No era declares it yet; the field is there so a table *can* say it.
- **The form only trims to five steps**, so the shortest piece finnfolk can generate is a little over four minutes — too long for a lament. This entry said `growForm` for months and **there has never been a function of that name**; it is `buildForm`, and the floor is a literal `steps.length > 5` inside it. Caught by the sweep, along with a second citation of the same ghost in `generate/tempo.ts`.
- **A rāga cannot be left mid-phrase**, which is what a thumrī does.
- **A sama'i changes metre in its fourth khana**; `beatsPerBar` is song-level.

## 5. Two checks that were measuring the engine, not the genres

**`the answer never doubles the tune at the unison or octave by accident`** was an engine-level residual present in every established genre at similar rates — measured over 200 seeds per genre: iskelmä 4/2335 (0.17%), metal 3/3570 (0.084%), rock 6/2930, country 5/2039, jazz 1/1757. It passed or failed at its own 40-seed sample substantially by luck, and four separate agents had to prove it was not theirs.

It is currently **green**, and it went green without being touched — which is the clearest possible statement that it was measuring a coin toss. If it returns, the fix is a threshold derived from measured output across the widened catalogue, not a loosened one.

**`brass sustains as well as stabs`** was measured at 19–20% against a >20% bar on the same afternoon in both directions. Genres that exclude the brass layer entirely — finnfolk emits zero brass notes — contribute nothing and cannot move it. Also currently green.

## 6. Closed

### From the list above

**§ Ghost notes.** `DrumPattern.ghosts`, a sibling of `voices`. Deliberately not a per-slot velocity map: level already has three owners — the metre, the section and the feel — and a number in a style table would outrank all three in every song. It says which strokes are ghosts and leaves how loud to the engine.

The interaction with `Feel.ghost` needed no coordination code, which is the good part. A written ghost is an ordinary event by the time the drawn pass runs, and that pass already refuses occupied slots, only ghosts odd sixteenths adjacent to a snare, and scales against the bar's mean snare velocity — which written ghosts are part of. So writing one *spends* it. Drawn ghosting around a written backbeat fell 94%, and a figure ghosting only the e leaves the a to be drawn. `GHOST_LEVEL = 0.28` was solved for rather than chosen: it makes a written ghost and a drawn one agree within 2%.

**§ A layer could enter and never leave.** `Chart.exits` alongside `enters`, so `playing()` is a window rather than a threshold. **The ordinal is placed, not drawn** — taken from the form's own section counts — so it consumes no random draw and no stream moved: across 2800 songs, no song came out with the same layers and different notes. It only fires in a section that states the tune, a rule that came out of measurement rather than design: without it a finnfolk kantele piece lost its pad from a melody-less bridge and the section went completely silent. Fires on 24.4% of songs, from 6.5% in ambient (every style writes `requireLayers: ['pad']`) to 35% in iskelmä.

**§ Nothing dropped out mid-section.** `Style.drops`, a weighted palette drawn **once per song**, and `generate/drop.ts`. A `DropSpan` is `FeelSpan`'s shape — half-open absolute bars — and it is IR, on `meta.drops`, because the layered-ambient goal needs the *composition* to be able to say which layers are muted for a stretch, and nothing in the IR could say it.

**Style-level with no genre half**, which is `breakCarrier`'s seam rather than `feels`': a fill palette is a claim about how a band in an idiom plays and travels; a drop is a claim about what one piece is made of, and the same genre holds `dub` and `nyabinghi`.

**The melody guard split in two**, and that is the interesting part. `Chart.exits` reads `here.includes('melody')` — a layer may only be taken away where something else is guaranteed to sound, and the tune was the only guaranteed something available. A drop removes layers for *bars* and may remove several at once, so one rule was doing two jobs. It is now a **named witness** per shape — `dub` is heard against the kit, `breakdown` against the wash — checked against `activeLayers` and named rather than searched for `breakCarrier`'s reason: from a pass that may not read a note, a tanpura drone and a walking bass are both `bass` with notes in it. The tune rule stays beside it doing the *musical* half. 0 empty sections across every style probed.

**Where it may land is placed, not drawn**, so the only random number in the feature is the one that picks the shape. Inside one section; both edges on the phrase grid; a whole phrase of band before it and a whole phrase after. That last rule is the answer to *may the return coincide with a seam* — **no**, because every transition kind edits the last bar before a join and two passes on one bar is the double-swing bug, and because a return landing on a section boundary is inaudible *as a return* when the arrangement changes there anyway.

**Nothing moved.** 807 songs — every style in all fourteen genres at three seeds — byte-identical in MIDI and Strudel against a copy of the live tree with only these hunks reverted. No style opts in, and `planDrop` returns before its first draw when there is no palette.

**One shortfall found by measuring rather than by reasoning, and fixed.** A four-bar drop needs a twelve-bar section, so a style whose sections are all eight bars places none at all — funk `minneapolis` 0/200 and iskelmä `humppa` 0/200, against 200/200 for reggae `dub` and jazz `bebop` on sixteen-bar forms. `minneapolis` is one of the two styles this document names as having asked for the feature, so the shape would have looked like it worked and done nothing. `Style.dropBars` is the fix, standing to `drops` exactly as `breakCarrier` stands to `transitions` — read, never drawn — and at `dropBars: 2` both reach 200/200.

**§ A style could not declare `Effects`.** `Style.effects`, merged `instrument > style > era > genre`, per key. The style goes **over** the era: an era is an average over a decade, a style is a member of that average, and an average does not overrule a member of itself. The decisive test is that under the other order the field would do nothing at all for `dub`, which is the only reason it exists.

**§ The hand drum.** Solved with a different shape from the one proposed here, and a better one. A `DrumSource` would have had to answer for the whole part at once, and a third of the patterns writing hand voices write kit voices in the same bar — funk's `congas`, latin's `cumbia-kit`, reggae's `roots-rockers`. Those are not a drummer choosing between two instruments, they are two people. So the split happens **on the voice**, in three tiers: `kit`, `hand`, and `either` for the auxiliary pieces that genuinely exist at both stations. That middle tier is what makes it work — every arabic `maqsum` is hand strokes plus a riq on `tb`, and with two tiers that `tb` would have conscripted a full acoustic kit.

**§ `Posture` had no floor value.** `floor` is a **conjunction**: `ArchetypeSpec.lap` says whether an object can be played from a carpet, and `FLOOR_SEATED` in `cast.ts` says whether this band sits on one — because the object under a funk conga player and the one under a tabla player are the same object. Two entries only, sitar and hand drum, and the restraint is the finding: 15 of the 23 archetypes indian and arabic stage are *borrowed* objects, so putting a concert vibraphone on a carpet stages the wrong instrument and puts it where it cannot be played. Riser goes to zero and the player moves downstage, since height and depth trade off from a camera 11 m out. `sitar.ts` had carried an apology recording that its boards-measured constants were shrunk onto a chair only because there was no floor posture; they are back.

**§ `concert-check.ts` only exercised four genres.** `CHECKED_GENRES = GENRE_IDS`. It immediately surfaced three real staging faults, all now fixed — see below.

**§ The sampled percussion was loaded and unreachable.** `SAMPLE_RACKS`, a table parallel to `BANK_VOICES`. **A rack is not a bank**, and the decisive reason is addressing: `.bank()` is implemented by prefixing, VCSL's names are bare, and a folder there is an *instrument* rather than a voice — `darbuka` is twenty recordings (five strokes × two velocities × two round-robins) and a voice needs a name *and an index*, which a bank table has nowhere to put. Racks ride on a machine and claim the auxiliary voices; there is deliberately no rack-only form. Levelled by measurement, because VCSL runs from −17 to −39 LUFS and a conga at unity is inaudible; the meter was cross-checked against numbers already in `source-levels.ts` (kick spread 13.0 dB against the recorded 13.2).

**§ A power chord was unexpressible.** A `'power'` `VoicingStyle`, built beside `chooseTones` rather than inside it — `chooseTones` does *selection* and its "the third goes last" rule is right and untouched, while a power chord is a **shape** built on a chord rather than out of one. Across metal's catalogue: tritones **23.4% → 0.1%**, perfect fifths **2.0% → 89.8%**, and a metric the original table never measured — comp onsets carrying a register-limit complaint, **19.2% → 1.2%**, because `voiceQuartal` is the one routine in that file that never consults `minInterval`.

**§ `generateDrumSolo` was hard-coded to a trap kit.** Orchestration now resolves from the style's own table through the same `drumStations` split casting uses. Four gestures generalise — state-then-answer, the run down the drum, the weight under the phrase, the run-in to the ending — and two do not: the hi-hat is a **limb** and `ARCHETYPES.handdrum` has no pedal, and a cymbal rings where a skin does not. A darbuka lands the band on a doum instead. A table naming both tiers solos on the kit, because those are two players. Arabic `dabke`, 16-bar chorus: **8 voices of trap kit in a takht → 4**.

**§ `applyShot` and the fill vocabulary were kit-only.** One literal in four places, found four times by the genre it was wrong for, and closed in two waves because each wave's sweep found the next site. `SeamOrchestration` in `generate/fills.ts` is the deliberate twin of the solo's — same `drumStations` read, same *the kit has first claim*, same *a gesture is a job, not an object* — and **six of the seven fill shapes generalise**. The one that does not is `cymbal`, the only shape in the vocabulary named after an object rather than a job, because there the object *is* the gesture: a bebop set-up is the ride ringing across the barline and a skin does not ring, so it is **re-aimed onto `lead-in`** rather than mimed. `snare-roll` needed re-*reading* rather than re-voicing — a kit roll is one surface struck repeatedly, and on a hand drum a roll is a tirakita, where the alternation produces the sustain a stick produces by repetition. Kit strokes in hand-table songs **1804 → 98 → 0**, the middle number being one gesture in a third file (`landEnding`) and the last one a fourth (`playShot`'s level read, which chose *how hard* from `bd` and `sd` and now chooses it from the station's own pulse and stating strokes).

**The fourth site outlived the ninth stray, because its symptom was an absence.** `KitVariation` is the *how it is played* axis against the pattern's *what is played*, and `HAND_VOICES` was `rd sh hh oh`. `handOf` needs its winner **present** and busiest, and a table of `lp mp hp tb` has none of the four in it — so this never wrote a wrong stroke and never could. It returned `undefined` in all 54 hand tables, in every section of every song, while every kit style in the catalogue got a verse thinner than its chorus. A check that counts wrong voices cannot see a gesture that never happened, which is the general lesson: *a hand-drum genre never sends for a kit* was green over this file for a day.

**What a hand is, decided by measurement rather than by taste.** `HandStation.keeps` is the cymbals and the brushes on a kit and never the kick, the snare or the toms, because thinning a backbeat is not a drummer varying a groove — it is a second band. At a hand station the same sentence picks out **the pieces on the stand and never the drum**. The catalogue says so in numbers: over the 54 hand tables, a row of four or more auxiliary strokes is evenly spaced **40 times out of 50**; a row of four or more strokes on the skin, **39 times out of 129**, averaging 3.2 strokes against the auxiliary's 5.2. The piece on the stand is a subdivision layer and the drum is a figure, in four genres written by four authors who never compared notes.

So **22 of the 54 hand tables get a hand and 32 do not**, and the 32 are the finding rather than the shortfall — every indian theka, every finnfolk frame drum, reggae's nyabinghi. Take alternate strokes out of a tīntāl theka and the result is not a sparser tīntāl, it is not tīntāl. The guard that enforces it was written for a hi-hat five genres away and needed no change at all: the hand must outnumber every voice outside `keeps`, and `jhala` writes `lp: 12` against `hp: 8`. **No station but the kit has a loud gesture**, so `lift` is optional: moving to the ride is a second cymbal and opening the hat is a surface that rings on demand, and what a riq player does at full tilt is *add* strokes — the one direction a mechanism that only removes and re-aims cannot go.

**Measured.** Arabic's auxiliary strokes per bar in an intro or outro **4.62 → 2.66**, against 5.25 in every other section, unchanged. 380 songs across all nineteen genres, MIDI and Strudel hashed against a copy of the live tree with only these hunks reverted: **25 differ and every one is a hand-table song** in arabic, indian, latin and finnfolk. Not one of the other fifteen genres moved a bit, which is the acceptance test — their tables never changed, so their music must not. **A genre with no percussion at all gets silence and always did**: `playShot` returns before writing a stroke when the bar has no drums in it, so classical's seam figure is the orchestra landing together and nothing on the `AkaiMPC60` that four eras name and none of them play.

**§ The `break` transition was hook-dependent.** The carrier is named rather than searched. The finding that shaped it: **nothing pitched is hook-invariant** — `--hook` moves the harmony, so every pitched layer follows the chords and the bass differed at 39 of 40 seeds. So no note may be read at all. The musical argument stands on its own: *"whoever filled the bar"* was already wrong with no `--hook` in sight, giving the same style a bass break in one song and an answering line in the next, and once producing a break carried by a single 32nd note 0.03 beats before the downbeat. Unstable seeds **53/1484 → 0**.

`Style.breakCarrier` followed, and closes the five residuals: indian's tanpura writes six notes into eight bars and has stopped three bars before the seam, so 43 of 10517 drawn breaks came out silent. Under `pad` — the śruti box, which is what is still ringing — that is **0**. `melody`, the tempting choice for a taqsim, is 24× worse.

**§ Nothing ramped the tempo.** `SongMeta.tempo` is a `TempoMap` — a list of `(beat, bpm)` breakpoints, **piecewise constant**, with `meta.bpm` kept and now meaning *the tempo the band counts off*. Three alternatives were argued and rejected in `grid.ts`: a function is unserialisable and makes each renderer pick its own sampling rate; linear interpolation turns beats→seconds into a logarithm that two implementations agree on to fourteen digits **and that MIDI cannot express anyway**, since a set-tempo event is a step; and a scalar-plus-ramp-description puts the curve in the reader, so every consumer has to implement the same easing. Piecewise constant is the only shape all four consumers implement identically, and it is what MIDI natively *is*.

The interesting half is the consumers. **MIDI is native and exact** — one set-tempo per breakpoint, 60 IR points → 60 events matching exactly. **Strudel cannot ramp at all**, established by reading the installed package rather than guessing: `setcpm` sets a scheduler global, the `.cpm()` pattern method is deprecated and is a constant time-stretch, and a per-bar `fast` bunches a bar's notes up and leaves a gap. The decisive reason not to attempt a trick is that `web/concert/transport.ts` recovers the sounding beat by inverting Strudel's scheduling equation, which is exact only because `cps` is constant — so a ramping audition would put every hand on the concert stage in the wrong place. A ramping song therefore emits a banner in its own source naming the curve and telling the reader to render the `.mid`. **This is the first shipping-only feature**, the exact mirror of the audition-only effects `midi.ts` has always had.

**The choreographer was deliberately left alone**, and that is the subtlest decision in the change. The stage animates against the audition, and the audition is flat; sizing windups from the tempo map would size them for a performance nobody in the room is hearing. Worse, handing `Board` the map without also ramping the transport would make *both sides* of the travel-speed comparison derive from the same wrong number — self-consistently wrong, which is the one failure a check cannot catch. So `concert-check`'s travel-speed assertion needed nothing and still means what it always meant: no hand exceeds human travel speed *at the tempo this show plays*.

Two shapes plus the identity: `accelerando` (linear) and `gathering` (squared, so half the speed arrives in the last third, because a linear climb has already lost the patient opening a qawwāli is built on). A **build** is left expressible and the reason it could not be built here is concrete — everything in `tempo.ts` runs before the form exists, because `buildForm` divides by the tempo to fit `Genre.duration`, while `planDrop` runs four hundred lines later; a build must arrive *at* the drop, so it wants a second planner appending to the same map, which is what a list-shaped IR was chosen for. **Drift** is fifteen lines and was not built because hiphop named no magnitude and a drift's entire content is its magnitude. A **final ritardando** was written and rejected: the ending already has an owner in `landEnding`, and two passes editing the same bar is how the double-swing bug happened.

Two latent bugs surfaced on the way, both fixed and both bit-identical today: `web/concert/show.ts` computed a number's length as `songDurationSeconds(song) * bpm / 60` — beats to seconds and back to beats — so a ramping number would have ended an eighth early; and `web/main.ts`'s radio auto-advance used the written length rather than the audition's, so it would have clipped every ramping record's ending.

**§ A bass note can slide, and three genres now say so.** `BassHit.glide` is a `BassTone` rather than a count of semitones, because the destination is *the note the author already wrote* and the field's whole job is to absorb it — so the headline claim was that **adopting is a deletion**. Mostly it is. hiphop's `drill` and `gfunk` fall from 11 written onsets to 8, dnb's three styles from 43 to 38, and in every case the second of two struck notes becomes the first one's destination.

**But "adoption is a deletion" turned out to be a half-truth, and the counter-example is the best thing this wave found.** dnb's `neurofunk/talking` merges nothing at all: every stroke in it is fenced by a gate rest somebody switched on, so collapsing pairs would delete the style rather than express it. The field is spent *inside* strokes there instead, taking pitch continuity across joins from 2-of-6 to 4-of-6, with the onset count and every `at` unchanged. `jumpup`'s `bouncing` and `stab-bass` stay struck on the same reasoning and now say why — **a leap written as a glide is a dive-bomb**.

**The three-point contour, which was the open design question**, is spent as two notes rather than collapsed to one leg, and the argument is sharper than the compromise it replaces: the second note begins on the pitch the first arrived at, so the re-strike is an amplitude re-gate and not a pitch re-articulation — and never re-articulating the *pitch* is what a Reese actually forbids. `techstep/reese` is four notes covering two bars as one unbroken contour, 0→♭2→0→♭3→♭2, where it was six struck notes. Verified in generated output rather than argued: each glide's arrival midi equals the next note's start midi.

A related limit found and worked around rather than papered over: the field cannot begin a glide *late*, since travel starts at the onset and `NoteBend` explains why that is forced. So "hold, then lift" is unsayable in one note — and sayable in two, which is what `techstep/held-reese` does, the pedal carrying no bend and the next note carrying the leg.

**§ A guitar bends into a note.** `Instrument.bend` says how readily a fretting hand pushes a string up to pitch, and `ornament` in `generate/solo.ts` spends an ornament draw on a bend instead of a grace note when that hand is on the line: the note is struck on the scale step below and carries a `NoteBend` up to the pitch the rules already passed. Electric guitars sit at 0.7 to 0.9, the steel-string acoustic at 0.3, the jazz box at 0.25 and nylon not at all. The coin runs on its own `${seed}:bend` stream, so a song with no bending player is byte for byte what it was. Measured over 288 songs in twelve genres, 51 change and 389 bends land. Rock bends 151 of its 1,476 solo notes and metal 86 of 3,262, because a metal solo is sixteenths and a bend needs a note over half a beat long; jazz, pop and finnfolk bend nothing, since their solo `ornament` is zero or their lead is never a guitar. Two things came with it. The twin lead is harmonised where the bent note *arrives* and bends with it, and `isolateBends` keeps every bend alone on its track, since a MIDI bend moves the whole channel: two of the first 687 bends had another note sounding under them, a note held across a 7/8 seam and a fingerstyle tail on a comp solo, and now none do. The one song that changed without a bend is a hiphop number whose picked bass carries glides, because a bent note is no longer shortened by the hand on any layer.

**§ A strum sweeps the strings.** Every chord in the catalogue was struck as a block, on a guitar as on a piano. `NoteEvent.rake` says how long after its chord a string is struck, in beats, and `rakeChords` in `generate/technique.ts` writes it for the two techniques that cross strings: `strum` at 11 ms a string and `plectrum` at 6, the pick all downstrokes, the strumming hand down on its own stroke grid and up between, low string first on the way down. Declared rather than written into the onset, so a chord still reads as one event to everything that groups notes by beat, and the spacing closes up at speed so the last string never leaves the chord's slot: over 160 songs none did. The .mid strikes each string on its own tick. The audition could not, because a slot writes one token and takes one nudge, so a raked track is dealt into one pattern per string, `comp — steel guitar (string 2 of 5)`, sharing the chain and the orbit; a track with no rake is one pattern and reads as it did. Over 16 songs a genre, country rakes 1,437 of 3,989 chords with a 22 ms median sweep and metal 9,875 of 18,777 at 6 ms, and a comp with a strumming hand costs one pattern per voice.

**§ A guitar runs where a harp breaks chords.** Six guitars shared `plucked` with the sitar, the kantele and the upright bass, an idiom that prefers a broken chord to a scale run because a harp's strings are tuned to one chord. A fretted six-string is played in scale positions, so it has an idiom of its own now, `guitar`, at run 0.9 and arpeggio 0.4 against `plucked`'s 0.4 and 0.8; the nylon guitar stays `plucked`, since a bossa or a classical study does break chords. On its own the field moved a guitar solo's run share from 22.9% to 24.2% over 160 songs, because the solo engine read the idiom's `arpeggio` and never its `run`. It now rewards a step taken the same way as the last one by `1 + run × 0.5`, which is the melody engine's own run bonus arriving in the one place it was missing: guitar solos run 27.3% of the time, every other lead's solo moves from 11.0% to 11.5%, and the tune is untouched.

**`glideTime` splits the genres exactly as its doc predicted**, which is the useful confirmation that the number deserved to exist — and every author solved for it against a real device or a real tempo rather than picking a feel. dnb writes none at all and takes the default of the whole note, because "the movement is the sound". hiphop writes `0.25` on drill — at 136–148 BPM that is 210 ms of travel and 630 ms holding, the report's own *bends across half a beat and then holds* — and `0.4` on g-funk, where at 90–102 BPM it lands on the 375–500 ms a Minimoog's glide knob sits at. **House's is the sharpest**: `acid` takes `0.25` because a 303's slide is a fixed RC time of about 60 ms, and at 120–132 BPM a slid pair of sixteenths runs 227–250 ms, so a quarter of it is 57–63 ms — the machine's own number, holding across the tempo range. `speedgarage` takes `0.5` because half of a four-sixteenth note is two sixteenths, so **the destination arrives on exactly the slot the deleted onset stood on**: the contour is unchanged to the sixteenth and only the second attack is gone.

**Measured**: hiphop 43.6% and 34.7% of bass onsets travel in the two styles and 0% in the other 22; dnb 73.9%, 47.8% and 24.3%, 0% in the other 21; house 12.5% and 18.2%, 1.97% genre-wide, with the per-song rates coming out at exactly the figure weights. **The §1.3 span trap was avoided by construction in all three genres** — every destination is a pitch the figure already struck, so no shape widened and `a riff is the same shape over every chord quality` stays exact at 45,120 notes over 371 figures.

**Three refusals inside the adopting styles**, which is the finer grain this wave found. house's `303-sparse` holds its source pitch for two steps, and a 303 slide is a step-to-step switch whose source is one step long by definition — so absorbing the pair would delete the held pitch rather than the restrike. `speedgarage`'s dips at slots 3 and 11 are movement at the *far* end of a note, which `NoteBend` refuses on renderer-agreement grounds, and adopting anyway would mean the bar never sits on its root. And `sub-and-skank`'s one adjacency would put the arrival on beat 3 under the kick.

**One pre-existing fault the glide made audible rather than caused.** `swing: 0.14` pushes `speedgarage`'s dips late enough to overrun the note behind them, and `render/midi.ts` marks any file where a bending note overlaps another, because a channel bend drags the overrun. Measured over 60 songs: **75 overlapping bass pairs before the change and 79 after** — the collision is the feel pass and is as old as the table. The glide only makes the `.mid` say so, and the audition is unaffected because a pitch envelope is per event. Documented in the style rather than fixed in the feel pass, which is the right call: two passes editing one bar is how the double-swing bug happened.

**§ Nothing subdivided below a sixteenth.** `DrumEvent.roll` — *this stroke, sounded as n even strokes filling the sixteenth it stands on* — with `DrumPattern.rolls` as the authoring end, a `Record<slot, count>` per voice beside `ghosts`' `number[]`.

**The IR could already hold the beats, and that is exactly why nobody noticed.** `DrumEvent.beat` is a plain float, so three onsets 36 ms apart were expressible before this existed; what could not exist was anything that would **read** them. Every consumer collapses a slot deliberately and each one is right to: `slotOf` rounds to the nearest sixteenth, `drumDynamics` says out loud that *two strokes on one slot sound as one stroke*, and the choreographer buckets on `quantise`. Two events a hair apart on one voice is a **collision**, and absorbing collisions is a service those passes exist to provide. No arrangement of floats distinguishes a collision from a roll, so the difference had to be *stated* — which is `NoteBend`'s shape one layer down and for its reason: a glide is not a second note carrying a flag, it is a field on the note saying the second note is what it absorbs.

**Both renderers play it, and both were checked rather than assumed.** The audition writes a **nested group**, `[hh*3]` where `hh` stood; `@strudel/mini` queried directly returns 0.7500 / 0.8333 / 0.9167 for the fourth slot of a bar, so the count is a divisor and the arithmetic is rational rather than sampled. The `.mid` is the *more* faithful of the two by construction: PPQ is 480, a sixteenth is 120 ticks, and 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 and 12 all divide it exactly — a trap triplet is 40 ticks with nothing to round. **The velocity grid needed no change at all**, which was the one thing expected to be awkward: a control pattern is applied appLeft, so all three strokes read the single number standing on their slot. Nesting it would have been the trap, because a `_` after a nested group stretches the *group* rather than holding its value and the two grids slide apart by a 32nd.

**One thing was genuinely broken and was found by parsing the output rather than by reading the code.** The MIDI note-off gate is a flat `PPQ/8` = 60 ticks, harmless for a decade because on channel 10 a key is a one-shot — and fatal the moment one key is struck twice inside that window. A roll of three puts the second stroke 40 ticks in and the first stroke's off at 60, so a reader that honours note-offs receives on/on/off/off/on and kills the **middle stroke of the roll**: not a wrong sound, a missing one, in the file this feature exists to make correct, and invisible to anything counting note-ons. The gate is now the spacing where the spacing is shorter. Verified by parsing the emitted `.mid`: 1454 IR events → 1609 note-ons, the roll at beat 23.75 landing on ticks 11400 / 11440 / 11480, and **0 overlapping note-ons on any key** in the file.

**A number per slot in a style table, where `ghosts` spent four paragraphs refusing exactly that** — and the two are consistent, which is the design question this entry turned on. `ghosts` refuses a velocity column because level already has three owners: the metre, the section and the feel, and a table number would outrank all three in every song. **Subdivision has none.** `metricStrength` says how strong a slot is and not how divided, `intensity` scales a velocity, `Feel` leans and ghosts and drags, and `KitVariation` thins a row and moves it without ever splitting a hit. The test `ghosts` applies is *does this outrank an incumbent*, and here there is no incumbent — a generator deciding the count would be inventing an opinion rather than expressing one. The half still refused is the one `ghosts` refused: **every stroke of a roll is the velocity of the stroke it subdivides**, no taper and no second number. That is also what the gesture *is*, and this genre's own table had already said so about its machine-programmed half — *"drawn into a machine a step at a time, and a step has one velocity"*.

**§ The audition threw the swing away.** `.nudge()`, as a control grid on every melodic part and every drum voice: seconds against the sixteenth the note is written on, signed, and read by superdough as `start(eventTime + nudge)` with no effect on the pattern's structure.

**This was not on the list, and nothing on the list would have found it.** Every entry above is something an author tried to express and could not. This is the opposite — something the engine expressed correctly, all the way to the renderer, which the renderer then discarded. `applySwing` writes the swung eighth at 0.665 and `slotOf` rounds it to 0.750; the swing was *computed*, *stored* and *thrown away* at the last step, on **903 events in one medium-swing jazz song**, every swung note in it. Triplet swing played as a dotted-eighth shuffle, which is not a rounding error but a different genre.

**19 % of all onsets across nineteen genres were off the grid**, so this was never only about swing — `Feel.push`, `laidback` and every dragged backbeat were being flattened the same way. §3.18's second half is the entry that says *no per-stroke timing offset*, filed by rnb, and it was half right: there was no way to *author* one, and there was also no way for one to *survive*.

**A control and not a finer grid, and the measurement is what decided it.** The commonest off-grid offsets are 0.20, 0.10 and 0.92 of a slot — microtiming, not subdivisions of anything. Writing them as notated tuplets would claim the music divides the beat in ways it does not and would multiply every grid in the file to say so. **A performer's placement is not a smaller note value.** The notation keeps saying sixteenth and the playback says otherwise, which is what `nudge` is for and what a resolution bump could never have expressed at any resolution.

Bounded by construction: `slotOf` rounds, so the correction is never more than half a slot and is as often negative as positive. Which made it **the fourth stale vocabulary in `check-notation.ts`** — `SIGNED_CONTROLS` held only `penv`, and a nudge grid without minus signs in it would mean the swing had gone missing again.

**The reason it survived this long is the part worth keeping.** `npm run genres` has asserted since it was written that *swing has a triplet feel*, and it passed on every run, because it reads `meta.swing` — a number on the metadata object, which was correct the whole time. The check verified the intention. Nothing verified the output. Cost: +33 % on the audition's file size, which is what it costs to write down a fifth of the timing in the catalogue that was previously free because it was absent.

**§ …and the melodic half of §3.15, a wave later.** `NoteEvent.trill` / `NoteTrill` — *a neighbour in signed semitones and a count of strokes per sixteenth* — with `Genre.decorate` as the authoring end. Adopted by finnfolk, which reported nothing, because the question that found it was asked from outside: **can the fiddle play a grace note?**

**The answer was "only in a break", and that was the gap.** Ornaments existed and were good — `ornament` in `generate/solo.ts` writes a crushed grace note and a mordent — but they were reachable only through `generateSolo`. finnfolk's own profile sets the highest solo `ornament` figure in the project, `0.6`, and says in as many words that *there is no such thing as a blowing chorus in Finnish folk music*; it then has a `solo` section in **one arrangement in four**. So three strains in four came out bare in the genre whose entire proposition is that one player decorates a tune everybody already knows. Nothing was broken and every table was right. The mechanism was simply behind a door the music never opens.

**The trill had to be declared, and the first attempt to infer it is worth recording because it looked reasonable.** The idea was to let the generator emit the strokes as ordinary notes and have `buildNoteGrid` notice that several landed in one slot. It cannot, and `slotOf` is why in both directions: a stroke exactly half a slot along **rounds up** and lands in the next slot rather than the one it was filling, and a grace note half a slot early rounds up into its principal's slot where the only position left to give it is a *late* one. Both failures are silent and both produce a plausible-looking grid. `DrumEvent.roll` had already answered this a wave earlier — it states the count rather than leaving it to be recovered — and the melodic case simply needed the same answer with a pitch attached.

**Two renderers, one arithmetic, and the check compares them.** The audition writes `[c4 d4]` where `c4` stood, which is the roll's nested group with the strokes named instead of multiplied; `render/midi.ts` expands the same count into struck notes, because a `.mid` has no group to nest inside. Both **decline on the same condition** — a note a later pass has cut too short to hold two strokes is played plain — and they have to, because `npm run genres` asserts that the MIDI's double note-ons exactly equal the IR's same-tick unisons and a disagreement of one shows up as a failure.

**That check earned its keep twice in one afternoon.** It caught a `latin` soloist on an electric piano at 12 doubles against 11 unisons: `ornament` is shared by both paths now, the solo rotation can put a soloist on a **comp** layer, and a trill struck over a held chord tone is the same key twice. `NoteTrill`'s own doc had already said *never on a chordal layer* — the fix is that the trill is now off by default and the caller, which is the only thing that knows what track the line lands on, turns it on. Grace notes and mordents need no such permission: they are ordinary notes spliced into a line, and a line that can hold its own notes can hold theirs.

**And a fifth stale vocabulary in `check-notation.ts`**, which by now is less a coincidence than a property of the file. Slots were split on whitespace, which was safe for exactly as long as every slot was one word — `[hh*3]` has no space in it, by accident rather than design. `[c4 d4]` does, so every trill was torn into `[c4` and `d4]` and both halves reported as unrecognised: **320 correct bars called broken by the check whose whole job is to be believed.** The splitter is depth-counted now, so nesting deeper than one level costs nothing and cannot be the sixth.

**Nobody's hand is ever asked to retrigger, and that is the decision the whole feature rests on.** `rolls` is read only where `DrumTrack.source` is `programmed` — *a machine programmed a step at a time; no drummer, and it can play anything*, which is `DrumSource`'s own words and is a description of a retrigger. A preset `box` is refused on the grounds that already cost it the fill, the solo and the ghosts; a `kit` and an `electronic-kit` are refused because there are hands on them. **The number that settles it was written years away and for something else entirely**: `REPEAT_SECONDS.floor` in the choreographer is 50 ms between two strokes of one hand, and it is that short only because the second half of a double is the stick's own rebound. A roll of three at 134–152 BPM asks for 33–37 ms, indefinitely, from one stick on one surface; `BURST_SECONDS` puts the sustainable rate at eight strokes a second and this wants twenty-eight.

**So the choreographer needed no change — and unlike the tempo ramp, that is a proof rather than a policy.** The ramp left the stage alone because animating against a map the audition was not playing would have been *self-consistently wrong*, the one failure a check cannot catch. Here the audition and the stage agree because a rolled stroke is structurally unable to reach a staged pair of hands, and the claim was measured both ways: `npm run genres` now asserts **10,866 rolled strokes over 120 trap and drill songs and 0 on the 3 drawn with a person behind the kit**, and across 48 shows in four genres the choreography — every hand, foot, viseme and light cue, with the song itself removed — is **byte-identical**. The one show whose IR moved moved only in the music it embeds. Note that `concert-check`'s partition check could not have caught the mistake: one event is still one gesture, so it would balance while the sound had three strokes in it and the picture one. That is why the guard is asserted at the source.

**Adopted where it was reported, and refused twice inside the same two styles.** `trap`'s `trap-kit` accelerates out of the bar — the four consecutive sixteenths it already wrote, with the last two struck twice and three times, so the last beat runs **107, 107, 54, 36 ms** and arrives at the report's own number. `busy-hat` answers its own first bar across a `cycle: 32`. `drill-kit` takes one stutter on the last eighth and `late-snare` takes trap's run-in. **`open-trap` refuses** because `oneHatAtATime` deletes the closed stroke under its open hat and a roll leading into a hat already ringing is two ideas in one moment; **`dotted-drill` refuses** for the better reason — sixteen hats three slots apart is a dotted-eighth chain, three against four, and a retrigger inside one of its steps is a second cross-rhythm asking to be counted at the same time as the first. A stutter is a gesture *against* a plain pulse, and that figure has no plain pulse to be against.

**Placement is exact rather than probabilistic.** Of 33 trap songs drawing a sixteenth-hat figure, 31 roll and the two that do not are `electronic-kit` — the hand gate, visible in the measurement. Drill rolls in 45 of 60 songs, trap in 31 of 60, the shortfall in each being precisely the weight of the figure that refuses. A thinned section drops the roll on the hits the hand dropped and keeps the ones it kept, which is `varyPattern` carrying rolls the way it already carries ghosts; an **opened** hit loses its roll, the one place the two differ, because an open hat is a surface left ringing and a retrigger is that surface re-struck three times inside 107 ms.

**Nothing moved.** 2334 songs — every one of the 389 styles in all nineteen genres at six seeds — hashed in MIDI and Strudel against a copy of the live tree with only these hunks reverted: **7 differ and every one is `trap` or `drill`**. The other 387 styles and all eighteen other genres are byte-identical in both renderers. `roll` is placed and never drawn, so no random stream moved. `npm run check` carries a standing count — **235 rolled slots** in its 150-song sweep — because a grammar wide enough to accept a shape is not evidence the shape is being produced, and that file's whole history is of vocabularies drifting from the renderer's.

**§ `DrumPattern.cycle` was one number for the whole kit.** `DrumPattern.cycles`, a length per voice, overriding `cycle` for the rows it names. **This is `ghosts` and `rolls` a third time** — a `Partial<Record<DrumVoice, …>>` beside `voices`, read off the same slot indices, carried through `varyPattern` with the hand — and saying so is the design argument rather than an apology for it: a `DrumPattern` is a set of rows, `generateDrums` already builds that list explicitly and walks it once, and every property a figure turns out to have has been a property of a row. What made `cycle` whole-kit was that the loop reading it predated the row list.

**One line, and the cheapest entry in this document by some distance.** `cycle: figure.cycle ?? slotsPerBar` became `figure.cycles?.[voice] ?? figure.cycle ?? slotsPerBar`. Ghost rows carry their voice, so a ghosted kick drifts with the kick it belongs to and no clause was written to arrange it. The fill's clearing already exempted `bd` — *a drummer's right foot does not stop for a fill* — which was written for a bar-shaped kick and is precisely what a drifting one needs, since a foot that stopped for the fill would restart in a different wrong phase every section. `accentOf` reads `slot % slotsPerBar` and stays bar-shaped, so a drifting kick takes the weight of wherever it lands, which is what a foot running against a metre the hands are still stating actually does.

**One case the read-only pass could not have hit, because nothing had adopted.** A cycle on a *hand* voice would let `varyPattern` pour one clock into a row already on another. The clock now moves with the hand like the ghosts and the rolls, and the merge is **declined** where the destination disagrees — declining costs no draw, since `planKitVariation` has already drawn. The realistic case never reaches it: a hand is elected only from `HandStation.keeps`, the cymbals and the brushes, and the kick and the snare are in neither.

**The check was rewritten, because the old one could not have failed.** *"A figure on its own cycle drifts against the bar"* read `d.cycle` off one pinned jazz pattern and then **re-derived the drift from the table with its own copy of `cycleHits`** — asserting a property of its own loop, never once calling the generator. The exact fault named in its own docstring, the generator going back to walking bars, would have left it green. It now runs `generateDrums` with the fill off over **every voice of every pattern in the catalogue** and compares the emitted absolute slots to `{ base + at }` for that row's own resolved cycle, plus a liveness clause that some pattern has one voice drifting under another that does not. **All four ways of breaking it were built in a scratch copy and watched fail**: ignoring the map fails on djent's kick (32 strokes where the cycle wants 74); smearing the map across the kit fails on the *snare and the hat*; walking bars fails across jazz; and removing the adoption fails the liveness clause at `0 pattern(s) name a cycle per voice`. Three formulations of "drift" were tried and discarded first — a row with a symmetry in it comes home sooner than `lcm(cycle, bar)` says, and `drift-kit`, `ride-drift` and `hat-against-five` each defeat a different one.

**Measured, and the catalogue answered a question nobody asked.** 9 of the 103 patterns carrying a `cycle` land in more than one place in the bar; the other 94 are `cycle: 32`, a two-bar figure. **Seven of the nine are named after a single voice**, and two of those seven — `dotted-hat`, `hat-against-four` — are that voice and *nothing else*, so a third of dnb `halftime`'s sections play hats with no kick and no snare. Deleting the rest of the kit is what drifting one row of it used to cost. All nine are left alone deliberately. And the catalogue is **951 drum patterns**, not the 1,005 in `parts.ts` and `docs/rhythm.md`: 1,005 is exactly 951 plus the 54 hand tables, and both files are corrected.

**Adopted in djent, which is the style that reported it**, and the adoption is a deletion. `grouping-kit`'s `bd: [0, 3, 7, 10, 14]` was `[0, 3]` stepped by seven and restarted at the barline; it is `bd: [0, 3]` with `cycles: { bd: 7 }` now, renamed `seven-foot`. Bar one is unchanged note for note and bar seven comes home where the guitar does. **37 of 37 kick onsets land on a guitar onset, against 35 of 40 before** — both cycles are phased from the top of the section and both step by seven, so the two sevens agree at every stroke rather than beating against each other, and the five that used to miss were the reset. The kick reaches all sixteen slots of the bar against five, and its mean velocity falls 0.803 → 0.726 because the foot is no longer pinned to a group head. `half-time` and `double-kick` are shared with the rest of metal and stay bar-shaped, so the figure fires in **83 of 200 djent songs**, which is its 6-of-13 draw weight.

**Nothing moved, in two arms.** 1,945 songs — all 389 styles in nineteen genres at five seeds, 6.7 M events, both renderers hashed with the title normalised out — run from a frozen `cp -R` of the tree with only the changed files swapped in. Adding the field: **1,945 of 1,945 byte-identical**. Adopting it in djent: **3 rows differ and all three are djent**, which is the 2 of 5 seeds that drew one of the two bar-shaped figures instead. A separate 200-seed djent probe puts the rest of the picture on the record: 83 songs change, every one of them at the kick, and across all 200 exactly **one stroke moved on a voice other than the kick** — a landing crash became an open hat, because the per-hit jitter stream shifts under a row that now emits a different number of strokes. That is the adopting style's own business.

**§ The two hand-drum genres took the transition palette that was built for them.** The mechanism had been generalised for two waves and nothing used it, which is §7's whole thesis about why that is worse than not building it. **Indian adopted 12 of its 28 styles and refused 11**; **arabic adopted 5 of 21 and left 16**. Both authors sorted rather than sprayed, and the sorting is the deliverable.

**The decisive measurement in indian was the figure each tāla resolves to.** Ādi at `[0, 8, 12]` and rupak and misra chāpu at `[0, 6, 10]` give **three onsets — a muktāyi spelled out, free, from the metre fallback**, which is what makes a `shot` and a tihai the same object rather than two things that rhyme. As shipped: 4,539 shots and 1,630 breaks over 5,600 songs, **16.0% of seams**, every adopting style placing a gesture in 139–200 songs of 200, and **0 kit strokes** throughout.

**The eleven indian refusals come in four shapes**, and one of them is a warning worth keeping: for the four styles with no cycle to land on — alap, jor, alapana, tanam — a palette is **not a harmless no-op**, because the band half of `playShot` moves the pitched tracks in 14 to 32 songs of 40 even where the drums have nothing to say. The others are the arrival belonging to someone who is not the ensemble (ghazal's radīf, padam's dancer, thumrī's bend inside the phrase), the form existing in order not to display (bhajan, dhun), and **one honest mechanical refusal left standing**: dhrupad and vilambit are on ektāl, whose sixes give six onsets where `bandHeads` defines a shot as two to four. A `shots` table would fix it, and the author declined to invent a chautāl tihai they had not measured.

**Arabic's five are each argued from a different gesture.** `samai` takes a `shot` above `fill` — the only style in the genre to weight it that way — because a taslim is arrived at by a **qafla**, and its comp falls 12.07 → 7.84 onsets while melody, pad and bass all rise: four layers converging, which is what a qafla is. `dabke` takes `break` at 4 because its own header says the bass is the loudest thing in the room and the break carrier is the bass — break bars drop drums 10.51 → 2.03 and comp 4.79 → 0.09 while **the bass holds at 3.94 either way**. `zaffa` refuses `break` because it is played walking and a procession cannot stop dead. And two styles **refuse everything**: `ayyub`, whose zar thesis is that nothing changes — the same refusal §7 records for hiphop's `bounce`, reached independently by another author — and `taqsim`, where a shot drags the improviser onto the sixteenth grid and a break deletes them outright, leaving a bar with no attack in it.

**§ There was no archetype for a vocal group.** Built, and then — the half that matters — **adopted, refused and measured by three genre authors working separately**, which is the pattern §7 argues a mechanism is worth nothing without.

The gate is a year and the year is 1970, not the year the first polysynth shipped. **Country restored both eras it had lost**: `stringband` puts the quartet back at the head of its pad table but at 6+4 against the pump organ's 5, not its old 9-in-13 — the organ was installed as a substitute and kept its place on its own merits, and what gave weight back instead was `strings1`/`tremoloStrings`, a sixteen-piece bowed section being a longer reach from four people and a fiddle than a parlour organ is. `honkytonk` went back to *exactly* its pre-removal table, because the 1955 music never changed and only the staging did. **Latin restored `conjunto`** — the coro at 5, ahead of the violins at 4, because the violins belong to the charanga next door and the era is named after the band that has none.

**Measured through the real `castSong` path, not the mapping**: stringband 110 of 211 pads draw a choir and **399 singers reach the boards as `vocal-group`, none as `synth`**; honkytonk 88 of 340 and 323 singers; conjunto 88 of 244 pads, 318 singers, 88 of 88 numbers. `npm run concert` now names `country#0 vocal-group/vocal-group` as its closest placement at 1.04 m, which is four people at one microphone.

**Two of the five sites refused, and the refusals are the better half.** Latin's `orquesta` is the first era in that genre with a section that can hold a note — a septet has nothing that sustains, which is *why* its coro is the only harmony in the room, and five saxes plus four trombones are the held chord under a mambo. Its cost is named rather than glossed: `chachacha` is joint-heaviest there and its refrain is sung, but that is an answer shouted between the band's own entries, not a chord held under the number, so `pad` is the wrong slot for it.

**Jazz refused after proving it would work**, which is the more useful shape of refusal. The brief said `swingera` had lost a choir entry; four revisions of that file exist and *"choir" appears in none of them* — there was nothing to restore, and the one hit in the folder's history is the scat singer's GM 52 in `vocals.ts`, a different layer. The real question stood anyway, and was answered with numbers: patched in, `choirAahs` draws 53 times in 300 songs and casts as a vocal group 53 times out of 53. It is refused because a big band's sustain **is** its sections — the pad measures a median 3.95 beats and 1.23 notes per bar, and `brassSection`, `strings1`, `clarinet` and `trombone` are all people already on the risers holding a note. What the Boswells and the Modernaires actually did was carry the *tune* for a 32-bar chorus, and no layer in the engine can say "for one chorus". Decisively: at one sung number in three, **only 15 of those 53 songs have a singer on stage at all**, so the other 38 would put four people at one microphone holding an open /a/ behind a clarinet solo with nobody out front.

**§ Five scale rows.** `durga`, `malkauns`, `hansadhwani`, `nikriz` proper, and the six-note `majorBlues` that country and rock both wanted and neither could spell. Four more were argued for and **rejected**, which is the more useful half: the altered scale is already melodic minor a semitone up and *"that is how the players think of it"*; nothing in `ChordQuality` could ever select a lydian dominant, so the chord quality would have to come first; `prometheus` is one composer's chord and `wholeTone` already holds that job.

**§ `Effects` had no envelope follower, so sidechain was unsayable.** `Effects.duck` — **decibels of gain reduction under the kick** — plus `duckBeats`, how long it takes to come back. Two keys on an interface that already merges instrument-over-style-over-era-over-genre, so the resolution came free and the whole feature is two fields, two renderer passes and a year gate.

**The entry asked for the wrong object, and noticing that is most of the work.** An envelope follower is a *runtime* device: it listens to one signal and derives a gain. Nothing needs to listen, because **this generator knows every kick onset at compose time** — so the follower's job is already done before a renderer starts and what is left is a declaration. That reframing is what turned a hard feature into a small one.

**The audition has had a sidechain the whole time.** `duckorbit` / `duckdepth` / `duckonset` / `duckattack`: an orbit is superdough's mixer bus, and a hap carrying `duckorbit(n)` schedules a ramp on *that bus's output gain* at its own onset. Strudel's own control documentation calls it *"a 'sidechain' like effect"*. Found the way `slide` was found to be dead and `getPitchEnvelope` found to be alive — by reading the installed package rather than recalling what Strudel can do — and it was worth it in both directions, because the obvious candidate is useless and the live one was not on anybody's list.

**Two mechanisms were rejected first and the second one is the interesting refusal.** `.compressor()` cannot do this and never could: superdough builds a `DynamicsCompressorNode` and a Web Audio compressor has exactly one input, so there is nowhere to put a key signal — it is the *device* a sidechain is made of and not the mechanism. `.tremolosync()` is the near miss: an LFO on a part's amplitude at a rate in cycles, with `tremoloskew` to make it a sawtooth, which is a pad pumping four times a bar in one line with no second part involved. It is refused because **an LFO is not keyed to anything**. Pop's own `kick-gap` figure puts the kick on slots 0, 3, 6, 8 and 11, and a four-per-bar sine under that agrees with the drums twice a bar and argues with them three times — a duck landing where the kick is not is a *wrong* contour rather than a missing one, which is the exact distinction the tempo ramp's banner turns on.

**So this came out `NoteBend`'s way and not the tempo ramp's, and that was not obvious in advance.** Both renderers play it and neither lies. The audition ducks a bus keyed off the kick's real events; the `.mid` writes an automation lane. Different mechanisms, one line — which is §6's own standard, *between a shape both renderers play identically and a shape one of them lies about, take the first.*

**The .mid's half is CC11, and it is the right controller rather than merely a free one.** Expression is **GM level 1**, so it sits with reverb send and pan rather than with CC74 and CC71 — which matters more here than it did for the filter sweep, since a device ignoring a brightness stream plays a slightly wrong timbre where a device ignoring this would play a record with its pulse missing. And it is defined as a percentage *of* channel volume: CC7 already carries `track.gain`, so a duck written there would have to know the mix level and multiply it, and a later mix change would silently change how deep the record pumps. Expression rides underneath and the fader stays the fader.

**The arithmetic is in `core/types.ts` and neither renderer owns it**, which is `sweptCutoff`'s argument with a stronger case: the two consumers do not merely need the same number, they need it in two units related by nothing obvious. superdough's floor is `1 − √depth`; MIDI's is the MMA curve `40·log₁₀(cc/127)`, amplitude proportional to the *square* of the controller — so a −9 dB duck is cc 76 and not cc 45, and the naive linear value would have shipped a duck half as deep as the one being auditioned. `duckFloor` is the one place decibels become an amplitude. **Measured across 24 ducked parts in generated songs, the worst disagreement between the two renderers is 0.075 dB**, which is the 7-bit controller's own quantisation and is asserted in `npm run genres`.

**A duck runs on the bus, which is why the held note is no longer the limit.** A control pattern is applied appLeft, so a pad's gain is sampled once at its onset and a supersaw held for four bars cannot pump inside it — the exact sentence both pop and house had written down as the reason their workaround got the comp and could not get the pad. The duck does not care whether anything was struck. It also ducks the reverb, because the ramp is on the orbit output downstream of that orbit's own reverb sum, so a pad's tail breathes with the pad instead of filling in the hole.

**Two costs, stated rather than discovered.** An orbit owns its reverb and delay, so a ducked part gets a *second* reverb built to the same `roomsize` rather than a send into the shared one — and `reverbGen` fills its impulse response with `Math.random()`, so the two halls are the same size and not the same room. `Space` says one hall with each player further back in it, and this is the first thing to bend it: a ducked part stands in an identical room next door. And the expression stream is not free — a ducked layer roughly doubles the `.mid`, 27 kB to 55 kB on a dance-pop record ducking two layers at four kicks to the bar. That is `BEND_STEP_CENTS`' price for `BEND_STEP_CENTS`' reason, and a duck is on every beat of every bar by definition where a glide is not. `DUCK_STEP_DB` is **1.5 dB**, above the isolated level JND and below what is noticeable inside a level already moving; at 1 dB the file was half again as large and the recovery moved 25 times a second instead of 17.

**One bug, found by parsing the output rather than by reading the code**, and it is the same shape as the roll's note-off bug. Sampling the whole duck window as one run steps clean over the fall, because the fall is a fiftieth of the recovery's length — so the first `.mid` written dropped 9 dB in a single message. That is precisely the click `DUCK_ONSET_SECONDS` exists to prevent, emitted by the renderer that was supposed to be honouring it, and it is invisible to anything counting controller messages: the stream was 5,127 messages before the fix and 5,124 after, because the missing points were being spent in the recovery instead. Only the *largest step between consecutive messages* showed it, at **8.92 dB against a bound of 1** — which is to say the file dropped the whole duck in one message and the check that would have noticed was the one nobody had written yet.

**`DUCK_FROM = 1993`, and it is a fact about the medium rather than a taste: you cannot sidechain on two-track.** A compressor keyed off the kick needs the kick on a channel of its own, so the technique is not merely unfashionable before multitrack but unavailable — the same shape of hard gate `SEQUENCER_FROM` and `DRUM_SOURCE_FROM` are. It separates pop's `gated` era from its `sidechain` one and house's `warehouse` from its `rave`, and nothing else in the catalogue. Applied by deleting the key in `effectsFor`, so a style drawn in the wrong decade comes out exactly as it would if the field had never been written.

**Adopted by seven styles in two genres, and the era table is deliberately not one of them.** Pop names its fourth era `sidechain` and it would have been the obvious place — and it is wrong, because an era is an average over a decade and this one carries `ballad` at 6, `chamber` at 4 and `indiepop` at 7. A torch ballad cut in 2005 is in that era and does not pump. So `Style.effects` outranking the era does exactly the job it was added for, one tier below the `dub` case that asked for it: pop's `dancepop` (pad 9 dB, bass 6 at a half-beat recovery), `europop` (pad 8, comp 5), `electropop` (pad 6) and `tropical` (pad 7); house's `trance` (pad 10 — the deepest in the project, on the one style where the wash *is* the pulse), `progressive` (pad 9, comp 6) and `frenchtouch` (pad 8).

**Placement is exact rather than probabilistic, which the year gate makes possible.** Every song of an adopting style drawn in the era that names the technique ducks its pad: 167/167, 156/156, 170/170 and 167/167 in pop; 194, 192 and 192 of 200 in house, where the shortfall is precisely the `warehouse` draws. `npm run genres` asserts it as an equality over 240 songs, along with three companions — that nothing ducks before 1993 (0 of 47), that every emitted audition pairs an `.orbit` with a `.duckorbit` (283 buses, 0 unmatched), and the decibel agreement above.

**The refusals inside the adopting genres are the finer grain.** `dancepop` leaves its comp alone because `piano-offbeats` and `pluck-eighths` already write the rise across the beat in `CompHit.vel`, and stacking a gain envelope on a velocity shape saying the same thing is the double-swing mistake in a different unit. `electropop` ducks the pad and refuses the bass on the opposite ground — there the bass is the *hook* rather than the floor, and pulling a riff down on every kick puts a hole in the middle of the tune. `tropical` refuses its pluck because the style's own header says a plucked instrument has no held notes in it, and a duck reaches what holds. House refuses twenty-one styles in three shapes: the warehouse half is gated by the calendar; six are built out of short events with nothing sustaining to duck; and `dubtechno` refuses on the strongest grounds of the three, because the space between its chords is already owned by a delay at 0.62 feedback that its own table calls the piece — and the audition's duck runs *after* that delay, so it would chop the echoes the style exists for.

**dnb was considered and never asked**, which is the cheapest refusal in this document: the genre's four files contain no mention of sidechain, ducking or an envelope follower, and its kick is not on the floor. A duck needs something held to pull down and a pulse worth pulling it down *for*, and a dnb record's pulse is the break.

**House's own note was wrong about house's own tables, and finding out is the §7 lesson again.** `house/index.ts` said the velocity workaround *"gets the comp and cannot get the pad"*. It gets the comp where the comp is struck; where it is held it gets nothing at all — and the two heaviest comp figures in the two styles that paragraph names are exactly that: `held-supersaw` in `trance` at weight 6, two onsets a bar at 0.66 and 0.64, and `long-chords` in `progressive` at weight 6, at 0.6 and 0.58. Flat. So on more than half the songs of those styles the mitigation everybody believed was covering the comp had never applied to it. That is why `trance` and `progressive` duck their comps and `frenchtouch` and every house style with a struck comp do not. Pop's era table carried a stale citation of the same kind — `sidechain`'s header said `index.ts` listed the gap "under what the engine cannot express", and there has never been such a section in that file.

**Nothing moved.** 2334 songs — every one of the 389 styles in all nineteen genres at six seeds — hashed in MIDI and Strudel against a copy of the live tree with only these hunks reverted: **37 differ and every one is one of the seven adopting styles**, in both renderers on all 37. The other 382 styles and all seventeen other genres are byte-identical. No random stream moved, because the duck is placed rather than drawn — it is a table entry read at effect-resolution time and consumes no draw, and that is asserted rather than argued: across **280 songs of the seven adopting styles, the notes, the kit, the sections and the tempo are identical** to the same seeds on the reverted tree, and the only thing that differs in any of the 37 is the effects. `npm run check` carries a standing count, deliberately a small one: seven styles of 389 means a 150-song rotation meets one or two, so it reads **2 buses from 1 kick** and what it asserts is the *pairing* rather than a rate, since a bus with nobody ducking it and a kick ducking nobody both play and neither pumps.

**§3.5 A filter could not move inside a note.** `Effects.filterEnv` — `{ octaves, shape: 'swell' | 'wah' }`, how far under the part's own cutoff a note begins and what the filter does from there. Adopted by funk's 1975 and 1980 eras on the bass. Audition-only, the seventh field in that interface so marked.

**It is not a new field beside `swell`; it is `swell` widened to hold both, and that is the whole design.** The hazard was named before the work started and it is real: a swell and a wah both write `lpenv`, and `render/strudel.ts` documents that two calls of one control on one pattern means the second silently wins. Two fields would have made that *representable* — `effectsFor` merges genre under era under style under instrument **per key**, so an era declaring a swell on the melody and a style declaring a wah on it produce one object carrying both, two `.lpenv()` lines, and a lead that plays whichever the emitter happened to write last. One key makes it unrepresentable: the later table replaces the earlier one whole and exactly one gesture survives the merge. **The exclusion is structural rather than enforced by a check**, and it cost two table lines in `synth/eras.ts` — the only writer the old field had — whose emitted audition is byte-identical afterwards. It is also the honest model — superdough builds exactly one filter ADSR per part in `createFilter`, and `lpenv`, `lpattack`, `lpdecay`, `lpsustain` and `fanchor` are five knobs on that one envelope. A swell is it with the sustain at 1; a wah is it with the sustain at 0. There was never a second device.

**The refusal beside it is worth more than the adoption, and it was measured rather than assumed.** The *other* thing "wah" means is a pedal rocked through the bar, and superdough has the machinery: `lprate`, `lpsync`, `lpdepth`, `lpshape` and `lpskew` are all registered controls in `@strudel/core`, all wired to an `lpf_lfo` node, and it is genuinely a **pedal rather than a per-note retrigger** — `lpsync` sets `rate = cps * sync` and the worklet takes its phase from `time = cycle / cps`, so the movement is continuous across the bar and every note reads the pedal wherever it happens to be. That is exactly the gesture funk's `wah-chank` is currently writing in velocities. It is refused on two findings from reading the worklet:

- **It has no anchor.** `createFilter` reads `anchor` only inside its envelope branch, and the LFO is summed onto the cutoff symmetrically about it — at `lpdepth(1)` the sweep runs from half the cutoff to one and a half times it. Half of every rock would be brighter than the decade, which is the one rule `NoteEvent.brightness` states in its own words.
- **It is linear in hertz and the ear is not.** `lfo-processor` computes `(waveshape + dcoffset) * depth` and adds it to a frequency parameter, with `curve` fixed at 1 by `createFilter`. A triangle is uniform in time, so across a wah pedal's own two and a half octaves it would spend **70% of every rock in the upper half of the travel measured in octaves** — an up-wah, permanently. The envelope path has no such problem: `createFilter` calls `getParamADSR` with `'exponential'`, which ramps linearly in log frequency.

**The thing `swell` had to suppress is the thing the wah wanted, which is the finding that made this small.** `getADSRValues` defaults a filter envelope to `[0.005, 0.14, 0, 0.1]` — attack 5 ms, decay 140 ms, **sustain 0** — and `effectChain`'s docstring has described that for as long as it has existed, as the "blip" that `lpsustain(1)` exists to defeat. An envelope filter's decay knob lives on that order, so what `swell` spent three controls defeating was a factory Mu-Tron. So the wah is one emitted line and two constants, `WAH_ATTACK_SECONDS = 0.01` and `WAH_DECAY_SECONDS = 0.14`, both in seconds rather than beats for `GLIDE_SECONDS`' reason: a pedal is a box with a knob on it and does not know how long the bar is.

**The adoption is `EraProfile.effects`, and the argument is a date.** The Mu-Tron III is a 1972 box. In 1968 it does not exist and funk's bass is a Precision through an Ampeg, so `jb` says nothing; by 1984 the bass is entered on a grid — `electro` writes `sequenced: { bass: 0.4 }` — and an envelope filter with no player's attack behind it is a filter envelope on a sequencer, which is a different record. The gap between those dates is exactly `pfunk` (1975, **2 octaves**) and `boogie` (1980, **1.5**, shallower because the palette puts `slapBass` and `slapBass2` at the top and a popped string is already a transient — a full-travel envelope on top of one is two attacks on the same note). This follows synth's own precedent, which writes `glide` and the swell on an era rather than a style.

**The numbers are arithmetic against funk's own table rather than a feel.** The genre's `effects.bass` cutoff is 1600 Hz and `fanchor(1)` puts the envelope's top there, so two octaves rests the filter at 400 Hz — both ends inside a Mu-Tron's own sweep, which is why the number is 2 and not 3: three would rest at 200 Hz, below the box. On the median funk bass note, MIDI 41 at 87 Hz, that is **four harmonics closed and eighteen open**, and back, inside 150 ms.

**And it fires, on a denominator that is checked rather than claimed.** Across 300 funk songs: **139 carry it — 71 of 71 in the 1975 era and 68 of 68 in 1980, 0 of 80 in 1968 and 0 of 81 in 1984** — over 48,677 bass notes, and never on a layer other than the bass. Median bass note 294 ms against an envelope 150 ms long, so the filter is home again with a third of the note left to sound on it; the 8.5% of notes shorter than the envelope get the front of the shape, which is what the pedal does to a ghost note too.

**The comp was refused and the arithmetic is the argument.** A wah pedal's own range tops out around 2.2 kHz. Funk's comp sits at 9000 Hz, so reaching a pedal's territory under it takes four and a half octaves, which would rest a palm-muted chank at 400 Hz — not a darker chank but no chank at all, 13.85 times a bar. The bass's 1600 Hz is the one ceiling in the genre's table a pedal fits underneath. That is `LAYER_RESPONSE` in `generate/filter.ts` read backwards: it puts the bass last because a lowpass closing on a part already below the cutoff *removes* it, and the same fact is what leaves the bass the only part here with room for a filter to move in.

**Audition-only, and the .mid is silent rather than approximate.** CC74 is GM2, is defined relative to the patch's own filter and can therefore only darken. A per-note quack could be spelled as a burst of it around each onset and would be a half-truth twice: the resting point would have to sit *below* the era's cutoff, so every `.mid` carrying one would play darker than the same song does today even between the quacks, and a GM1 device ignoring CC74 would drop the gesture and keep the darkening. `NoteBend`'s rule decides it.

**Nothing moved that should not have.** 19 genres × 8 seeds with vocals on, both arms generating 363,074 notes and 189,198 drum hits — so neither arm is a sweep of nothing — hashed in MIDI and Strudel against a frozen copy of the tree with the title normalised, because both renderers embed it. **Adding the field: byte-identical everywhere, both renderers.** Adopting it: **only funk moved, and only its audition** — across all 300 funk songs the `.mid` is byte-identical, which is what audition-only means measured rather than asserted. `npm run genres` carries both halves: that the wah reaches the emitted audition in the two eras that declare it and neither of the two that do not, and that **no part in the project emits two `.lpenv()` calls**, counted over the parts that carry one at all rather than over a rotation where the answer would have been 0 of 3.

**§ The gate, refused — and the refusal took three findings rather than one.**

The smaller half of §3.17, from rnb: `Style.effects` cannot say *gate*, `synthsoul`'s gated snare is spelled as reverb plus a hard lowpass, and `DrumTrack.voiceEffects` was named as the ready-made home for it. All three parts of that sentence turned out to need checking.

**There is no gate in the audition, and the reason is in four lines of `reverbGen.mjs`.** The impulse response is `randomSample() * decayBase^j` with `decayBase = (1/1000)^(1/decaySampleFrames)` — an exponential decay *by construction* — and the only envelope control over it is a fade-*in*. A gated reverb is the opposite shape: a dense, flat tail cut off hard, and its characteristic sound is the flatness rather than the shortness, which is why truncating an exponential decay at 200 ms gives a small room rather than a big gated one. Superdough will convolve against a supplied `ir` sample instead, and none of the three registered manifests has one.

**`@strudel/core` registers a `gate` control and nothing consumes it.** It is a control-voltage parameter with a TODO pointing at Tidal's hardware docs, and no `gate` is read anywhere in superdough — which is exactly the `slide` situation `NoteBend` found, a control that exists by name and would emit a silent no-op.

**And MIDI has no gate controller at all**, so unlike the duck there is no half of this that ships. Refused on both ends at once, which is the strongest form the answer comes in: it is not that one renderer would have to lie, it is that neither can say it.

**The third finding was the one worth acting on and is now closed.** `DrumTrack.voiceEffects` was read by `render/strudel.ts` and **written by nothing** — no genre, no era, no style, and `generate/song.ts` never set it. Measured at **0 of 380 songs across all nineteen genres**. Four separate files cited it as *"the field that exists for exactly this"* — pop's era table, pop's `stadium`, rock's era table and rock's own style — and none of them could reach it, because there was no `voiceEffects` on `Style`, `EraProfile` or `Genre` for an author to fill in. The home existed and had no door.

**The door is `voiceEffects` on all three tables, and `voiceEffectsFor` in `generate/song.ts` beside `effectsFor`** — genre under era under style, per voice and then per key, sharing the `DUCK_FROM` date gate through a `dated` helper that both now call. Sharing that gate is the point rather than tidiness: the year is a fact about the recording situation and does not care which drum voice a field was written on, so without it a table could put a duck on a 1952 bandstand by writing it on `sd` instead of on `drums`. Verified by forcing exactly that and watching `duck` and `duckBeats` come back off a 1968 era.

**Adopted in one era and one era only: rock's `arena`.** A field nothing writes is the thing this document keeps complaining about, and a field adopted everywhere at once is a field tested against no case. `arena` had written the compensation into its own table — 0.5 reverb on the whole kit under a 5.5 kHz ceiling, the largest number in the genre, held down to keep the tail off the hats — and that has been **undone rather than left standing beside the real thing**: the kit goes back to 0.3 and 8.5 kHz, between `hard`'s and `alt`'s, and the snare alone takes `reverb: 0.9, lowpass: 6000`. The emitted body now reads `.room(0.90).lpf(6000)` on `sd` and `.room(0.30).lpf(8500)` on all eight other voices, which is the sound four comments in two genres had described and none could write. Every other era in the catalogue is byte-identical; in `arena` the *music* is byte-identical too, measured at 40 of 40 seeds — every note, stroke and section unchanged, and only the mix moved.

**Still refused: the gate itself**, for the reasons above, which have nothing to do with authoring. `Effects` has a reverb and no decay, so what a table can now write is the plate on one voice. The tail is right; the stop is not expressible, and rock's two paragraphs now say exactly that rather than claiming the whole gesture.

**Built after all, on 2026-09-02, with a supplied impulse.** `Effects.gate` keeps that many seconds of a generated 300 ms noise burst — `gateBurstDataUrl` in `render/strudel.ts`, registered as `gateburst` from a data URL rather than shipped as an asset — and the part goes on a bus of its own, because an orbit has one reverb. The synth `digital` and `retrowave` eras and rock's `arena` write it on the snare. MIDI still has nowhere to put it.

**§ A drum solo was named after a kit whatever played it — the other half, which was not the half anybody was looking for.** `drumStations` closed the hand-drum half and closed it properly: arabic prints `darbuka`, indian `mridangam`, latin `congas`, measured over every drum solo in the catalogue. What it could not close is a question it is not asked to answer. **`drumStations` says which objects a pair of hands would need; it does not say whether there are any hands.**

So every drum solo whose drums are a *machine* was announced as an object with nobody at it. Measured over 100 seeds in each of the nineteen genres: **33 of 150 drum solos — 22% — named something that is not on the stage**, across six genres, funk 12 and dnb 8 of them. A Roland TR-909 chorus was a "drum kit". A Linn 9000 was a "hand drum". An 808 running a sampled mridangam was a "mridangam", and that is the worst of the three precisely because it is the most specific: it names an instrument nobody in the building is touching, in the tradition where the object is most of the point.

**Why it survived the first wave, and why that is the interesting part.** `roster` stages a percussionist only when `isPlayedByHand(source)`, so a box or a programmed machine stages *nobody*; `placeMachine` puts the box on the boards instead. Both halves of `cast.ts` already knew. `Section.solo.instrument` was resolved beside the *bank*, four hundred lines before the *source* was drawn, so the one field whose whole job is to name the object was the one place in the pipeline that could not see whether the object had a player. The fix is to resolve it after the draw — it consumes no random number, so no stream moved.

**A machine is named as the machine, with the whole bank string, rack and all**, which is not a second decision: it is exactly the string `placeMachine` already puts on the object it stands on the boards, and the argument for keeping the rack is that function's — a box loaded with a sampled darbuka is a box, nobody is sitting behind the drum, and dropping the rack would delete the only mention of something genuinely sounding and genuinely invisible. **`electronic-kit` stays a kit**, which is the same question answered the other way rather than an exception to it: a Simmons is pads that a drummer hits, and there is a person behind them.

**Nothing else moved, and the check is stronger than usual because the blast radius is a naming field.** 760 songs — all nineteen genres, twenty seeds, sung and instrumental — MIDI and Strudel hashed against a copy of the live tree with only this hunk reverted: **byte-identical, every one**. 1,903 cast placements, 163 staged machines, 352 follow spots, every venue, every cue count and every gesture count: identical. Of 982 solo lines, **16 changed, and every one of them is a machine**. `playerFor` falls back to the first player on the layer, so a changed string could only redirect a follow spot where two percussionists are staged — and this branch fires only where zero are, so `resolveSolos` drops the spot exactly as it already did.

**One thing found beside it and deliberately not fixed here**, because it is in `generate/` rather than at this site. Five of the 33 are `source: 'box'`, and `canVary('box')` is false — so `kitSolo` is `undefined` and `generateDrumSolo` is never called, while the section still records a solo, because that guard reads `active.has('drums')` and a box writes drum events like anything else. A preset box therefore announces a drum chorus it does not play. The two guards are four hundred lines apart and disagree; whoever fixes it should make the section guard ask `canVary` and not merely rename the result.

**§3.1 The ending and the count-in were genre-level.** `Style.ending` and `Style.countIn`, both overrides of the genre's field, resolved `style ?? genre` at three read sites — the same line `scaleForChord` has always taken. `EndingStyle` moved to `core/types.ts`, because `Style` names it now and `style/types.ts` cannot reach `genre/types.ts`: the dependency runs the other way, since a genre is a collection of styles. `LayerId` and `SectionKind` are in that file for the identical reason.

**The two read sites that had to agree became one function.** `withCountIn` decides whether four clicks go in front of the pattern; `counted()` in `web/concert/show.ts` decides whether the leader gives the count where there is nothing to click. Those two agreed for free while the answer was one field on a genre, and stopped agreeing for free the moment a style could override it — a number with neither cue starts on nothing, one with both starts twice. `countsItselfIn(meta)` is exported from `generate/song.ts` and both call it.

**What the wrong ending actually cost, measured by generating each style twice at one seed with only the field flipped.** Not the cymbal: all six styles exclude the drums, so the crash-and-kick branch never fired and never had. The cost is the pitched half, and it has three parts of very different sizes — every onset inside `LAND_WINDOW` is **re-articulated on the downbeat with its velocity lifted 15% and a further 0.06**, a few are *moved* there, and where a layer had nothing on the landing the recall branch **manufactures** an attack on a bar the music meant to leave ringing. Over 40 seeds each, notes lifted per song and then songs in 40 that gained an invented attack: classical `nocturne` **6.2 / 7**, classical `prelude` **4.9 / 12**, finnfolk `konserttikantele` **2.0 / 1**, `runolaulu` **1.8 / 1**, `itkuvirsi` **1.6 / 3**, `karjanhuuto` **0.9 / 0**.

**The first measurement of that was of the wrong number, and it is worth recording which.** Counting *notes on the final downbeat* — the obvious probe, and the one this document's own brief carried — gives figures of the same order and attributes them wrongly, because most of those notes were already on the downbeat. The button's work is the velocity and the re-articulation, not the position. The two probes only separate when you generate the same seed twice.

**`countIn` moves no audible bar and was adopted anyway.** `withCountIn` returns early on any number with no kit in it, which is all four finnfolk styles that wanted `false`, so no click was ever going to sound either way. What the field reaches is the stage: the leader was beating four in front of a lament, and is not any more. That is the whole of its effect and the style comments say so rather than implying an audible one.

**§3.2 `TitleContext` carried no key or mode.** Two fields, `tonic: Pc` and `mode: Mode`, at the one construction site in the tree. All three genres that reported the gap adopted, and **two of the three refusals were answered by code that predated them**.

- **Classical** refused on the grounds that the field *"would put a spelling decision (is it C♯ or D♭?) into a type"*. `spellingFor` and `keyLabel` in `core/pitch.ts` have made that decision for every song in the project since long before this genre existed, and `generateSong` calls `keyLabel(tonic, mode)` **three lines below** the call into the title generator. The type carries two numbers and no spelling at all. What is genuinely local is *typography* — `keyLabel` prints `Eb minor`, which is right for a filename and wrong on a programme — so `classical/titles.ts` spells the accidental out and leaves the choice of accidental where it has always been. **131 of 300 songs name their key, 131 of 131 correctly**, and all twenty-four keys were spelled out one at a time rather than sampled, because two string replacements are exactly the kind of thing that works on twenty-three keys and mangles the twenty-fourth.
- **Arabic** names an instrumental piece *form plus maqam*, and the maqam is a pure function of `(tonic, mode)` — **except on `taqsim`**, which overrides `scaleForChord` and is the one style whose two maqamat the genre tables cannot produce. So the title asks `style ?? genre`, the same resolution the melody gets. Over 300 songs the piece is in one of **seven maqamat across sixteen key labels** — Kurd 80, Hijaz 72, Nahawand 59, Hijazkar 41, Farahfaza 38, Nawa Athar 7, Ajam 3 — where a random draw from the eight would have been right about one title in eight. **52 of the 300 print one and 52 of 52 are correct.** It goes on the 88 form-style songs and on none of the 212 sung ones, because *Enta Omri* is not *Ughniya Nahawand*.
- **Indian** needed only `mode`, since `raga(major, minor)` picks by mode alone. The rāga is not looked up from a table beside the hook — `ragaOf` calls the style's own `scaleForChord` and reads the `ScaleName` back off the `Scale`, so the announcement is derived from the mechanism the melody uses and cannot drift out of agreement with it. **77 of 300 name a rāga, 77 of 77 correctly, 19 distinct rāgas.**

**The indian adoption found a second gap while filling the first, and it is the better half.** The same seven notes are two different rāgas *to say out loud*: a kṛti announced as *Rāg Yaman* is a Carnatic item introduced with a Hindustani name, which is the mislabelled-record failure `TitleContext` exists to prevent, arrived at from an angle nobody predicted. `RAGA_NAMES` in `indian/styles.ts` has two columns and the file's `TALA` table was already split the same way — ādi tāla and the chāpu tālas against teentāl and ektāl — so half the vocabulary had been tradition-aware for as long as the other half had not. **55 Hindustani names and 22 Carnatic ones in the sweep.** No invention was needed: every Carnatic name was already sitting in a comment beside the rāga constant it belongs to.

**Songs cannot move structurally, and that is what made the re-weighting safe.** The title draw is the **last consumption of the song stream** — no `rng` reference survives between that line and the end of `generateSong` — so a genre that changes what its title function draws moves its own titles and not one note of anybody's music. All three genres did change their draw counts.

**Nothing moved that should not have, proved three ways.** Two copies of the live tree with hunks reverted: `base` (everything reverted) and `mech` (the fields added, no genre adopting). Every style in the catalogue at six seeds — **2,334 songs over all 389 styles in all nineteen genres**, MIDI and Strudel hashed with the title stripped out of both, because both renderers write the title into the file and a title-only change moves both hashes.

- **Adding the fields moved nothing.** `base` and `mech` are identical on all 2,334, music hash and title alike.
- **Adopting §3.1 moved music on exactly the six styles that adopted**, and on nothing else: `nocturne` 6 of 6 seeds, `prelude` 6 of 6, and 5 of 6 apiece for `itkuvirsi`, `karjanhuuto`, `konserttikantele` and `runolaulu` — 32 songs of 2,334. The one seed each that did not move is a finnfolk song whose last bar had nothing inside the landing window for the button to touch.
- **Adopting §3.2 moved 155 titles and zero notes**: arabic 31, classical 84, indian 40, with **0 music-hash differences across all 294 arabic and indian songs**. The counts were checked before the comparison was believed — 2,334 rows per tree, 2,334 distinct music hashes, no empty ones — because a sweep that generates nothing diffs clean against another that generates nothing.

### What a stage could not say

The list above came from genre authors reporting what they could not express. Nothing produced a comparable list for the **staging**, and the reason is the finding: there was nowhere to look at it. So the faults below were not compromises anybody recorded — they were shipped, believed correct, and invisible.

**A costume bench.** `web/looks.ts`, served at `/looks`: every hair style, every accessory, **every accessory crossed with every hair**, every fabric, and every genre's wardrobe drawn through the real `castSong` path. It exists because `gallery.ts` — the *instrument* bench — opens with the argument verbatim: models were "verified against geometric assertions, and shipped, and the only way to look at any of them was to start a concert and hope the camera cut to it. Assertions catch what you thought to assert. A bench catches the rest." Sixteen hair styles, twenty accessories and fifteen fabrics had just been written under exactly those conditions. The bench found eighteen faults on its first run. Everything else in this section is one of them.

**Rooms had no builder.** `stage.ts` built one rectangular proscenium — boards, backdrop, wings, fly bar, curtain — with four boolean modifiers, and that was the whole vocabulary for fourteen genres. A concert hall, a courtyard, a barn, an arena and a dancehall were **architecturally the same box in different paint**, and the genre authors were not at fault: a `StageRoom` could carry colours, props and a fog number, so data was all the system could accept.

`web/concert/rooms/` now follows the pattern `instruments/index.ts` already set — a `Record<RoomStyle, RoomBuilder>` where each room is a file and a missing entry is a compile error. **Twelve** rooms — it was eleven when this paragraph was written, and the twelfth is `ballroom`, added the day after — and `stage.ts` fell from 751 to 460 and keeps the machinery the show drives, which is the line that had to be drawn correctly: **the stage is the part of the picture some other file already has an opinion about, and the room is the part nothing else does.**

Each author proved every *other* room byte-identical against a copy of the tree with only its own hunks reverted, walking world transforms, geometry attribute hashes, material descriptors and every `StageMetrics` field. The salon added negative controls — a 0.1 mm change to another room's dais, and a pure-geometry change with no metric change at all — to show the harness could see what it claimed to check. The seam's own bug surfaced immediately: `houseLid()` derived the house ceiling as `houseY + LOW_CEILING` for any room with a finite headroom, which was true of the cellar and nothing else, so a roofed courtyard would have hung its chandelier a metre below its own plaster.

**Every performer in every genre was the same lathe.** `Look.outfit` was four colours and a fabric. There was a `cut?: { lapel, shoulder, flare }` whose own comment said *"this is where a decade actually lives"* — **written by nothing and read by nothing**, and three numbers could not have made a thobe out of a lounge suit anyway. `Garment` is eight silhouettes now, dispatched from `performer-garments.ts`, with a `legsOf()` contract so the legs know whether there are trousers at all. **All nineteen genres carry a table** — fourteen when this was written, and the population reaching every genre rather than most of them is the mechanism succeeding, not the sentence expiring. Finnish folk is drapes over floor-length linen in 1780 and V-panelled waistcoats in 1860; arabic is robes and gowns where country is shirtsleeves and braces.

**Hair and hats could not see each other.** `buildHair` and `buildAccessories` were independent `void` functions adding geometry to the same head, so a hat sat at a fixed multiple of head radius whether there was a crop or a 42 cm afro underneath. Every hat in the union disappeared inside an afro. `buildHair` returns a `HairProfile` now — **measured off the built group rather than tabulated**, because a table saying "a beehive is 2.83 R" is true until somebody improves the beehive, which is this document's own recurring bug one level down. Head furniture reads it and either presses the hair or rides on it, bounded so that a bandana over an afro does not solve the fit by swallowing the halo entirely.

Four faults that were shipped and invisible:

- **The hood and the wrap had their face-hole over the right ear.** `SphereGeometry(…, phiStart: π*0.30, …)` — phi = 0 is −x. Front-on, a hooded performer was a featureless egg with two eyebrows through it. Live in four genres.
- **The fabric that exists to look expensive was the darkest thing on stage.** Nothing set `scene.environment`, so `lame` at metalness 0.90 had no diffuse response at all. Every `loudFabric: 'sequin' | 'lame'` lead — iskelmä, funk, latin, arabic — was the dimmest figure in the room. Nine of the remaining fabrics were also mutually indistinguishable at ten metres and needed a second axis, not a finer one.
- **Forty players in evening dress had no lapels.** They were `shade(jacket, -0.07)`, and `shade` clamps at zero, so on classical's near-black `#141418` every negative shift landed on black. The fix picks its direction from the colour.
- **Dressing one genre re-rolled every player's face.** `cast.ts`'s garment draw promised in a comment that it was *"last of every draw in this function — the same faces, the same colours, the same hats, in different clothes."* It was false by two calls: `skin` and `hair` were drawn inside the returned object literal, after it. Only findable by diffing the whole catalogue.

**The two that were still open here are closed.**

**§ The house floor was sized off the house.** `houseDepth + 8` centred on `lipZ + houseDepth/2` is a house-shaped number for a plane that has to reach past the house at both ends, and it pinned the upstage edge at `lipZ − 4` whatever the building was. Since `lipZ` and `backZ` are `±depth/2`, the ground ran out `depth − 4` metres short of the back wall in **every venue deeper than four metres, which is all thirteen dressings the two rooms are ever asked for** — 1.40 m in jazz, 2.30–2.60 m in iskelmä and ambient, 3.60 m in arabic's widest court. Both now measure from the building: 2 m past `backZ`, and the reach they already had at the house end, which was never the part that was wrong.

The courtyard is where it mattered and the proscenium is why it survived. A proscenium's tormentors exist to stop "a wide shot seeing past the arch into nothing" and the wedge they cover is exactly this one — but they are front-on geometry and orbit yaw is not clamped anywhere in this renderer. A courtyard has no masking at all, and its own walls run the full plan from `backZ − 0.1` to `houseBackZ`, so the paving stopped three and a half metres inside a court closed on four sides. **Proved by reverting only this in the live tree**: the same arabic frame comes back with its entire lower half black.

**§ `headphones` was one rigid object.** Every hat here rides tall hair by being picked up whole, which is what a hat does. Headphones are the exception in the wardrobe and in the world — the band goes over what is on the head, the cups go on the ears, and the two are joined by a slider whose whole purpose is to take up the difference. Lifting the group whole took the cups with it, and over the union's tallest heads that is **1.0 R of travel, about eleven centimetres**: not a fit slightly off, but the cups above the crown of the skull. The bench shows it plainly at `hat:headphones:beehive`, which is what settled it.

So the band rides, the cups stay, and **the ring stretches between them** — the slider drawn rather than implied. `seat()` is solved before the object is built rather than after, and the one sprung object in the union is the only case that reads the answer; the other nineteen ignore it and are right to. The arithmetic is what makes the stretch the right shape rather than merely a taller ring: the band crosses the cup line at `1.175 R` with no lift and at `1.065 R` at a full one, against cups at `1.06 R`. It meets them *closer* the further it has stretched, which is the direction a real slider goes, and at `lift = 0` both numbers reduce to exactly what was there before — so the unhatted picture is untouched by construction rather than by luck.

### Found while fixing the above

- **Two counts in `core/types.ts` and `synth/eras.ts` had gone stale where they stood**, both one line from the field being edited. `Effects`' own header said *"two fields are audition-only"* when six were, and the CS-80 paragraph justified putting `glide` and the swell on the era on the grounds that *"styles have no `effects` of their own"* — which `Style.effects` stopped being true of some time ago, and which §6 records two entries above. Neither was a bug and both are the thing §8 keeps asking for: a fixed limitation leaving its apology behind. The numbers stay on the era, now for the reason that survives — a ribbon controller and per-key aftertouch are facts about the keyboards on the stand, not about a piece.
- **`Fabric` was inert.** `clothSurface` derived sheen from colour *saturation* and never read `Look.outfit.fabric` — precisely the failure that field's doc says it exists to prevent. Wired at all six call sites with `fabric` required and the saturation path deleted.
- **Quartal voicings and degree subsets assumed seven notes.** Three scale steps is a fourth only in a heptatonic scale. This entry said the six-note `blues` was "already stacking fifths" and that was never measured: its three-step intervals are `[TT M3 P4 TT m6 P5]` — a **tritone on average**, with exactly one fifth in six. The fix was right and its stated reason was wrong, which is the more embarrassing of the two ways to be wrong.

  **Re-measured since, and the generalisation is better than the fix that prompted it.** Over 3,059 quartal voicings, the 160 built on a five-note scale — all hiphop — come out **97.5% perfect fourths**, against the old three-step path's 0% fourths and 61% fifths. That makes the pentatonic path the *cleanest* quartal in the engine: the seven-note path every other genre uses runs 84.9% fourths and 15.1% tritones, because a diatonic fourth on one degree of a major scale genuinely is a tritone. One case is accepted and unfixable — `majorPentatonic` has no perfect fourth above its tonic, so 43% of its stacks are major thirds; at three voices that is C–E–A, which is a C6 and the So What chord's own proportions. A rival implementation measuring semitones rather than counting steps was written and tested: **identical on all 161 non-heptatonic calls**, and worse on 739 seven-note ones, so `stepsNearest` keeps its name and its behaviour.
- **`check-notation.ts` duplicated the drum vocabulary as a string literal** and would have rejected `tb`, `lp`, `mp` and `hp` the first time a style emitted one. Now derived from `DEFAULT_DRUM_MIX`, whose keys the compiler guarantees complete.
- **`performer-look.ts` claimed a missing case was a compile error.** A `switch` in a `void` function is not checked for exhaustiveness, so a new hair style would have produced exactly the silent silhouette the header said was impossible. Both switches now end in a `never` assignment.
- **Two scale rows were mislabelled** — `hungarianMinor` as Maqam Nikriz (it is Nawa Athar) and `majorPentatonic` as rāg Durga (it is Bhoopali). Both caught by the genres that use those scales daily.
- **Four genre-keyed tables in `concert/`** became `Genre.staging`, so a genre owns its own room, wardrobe, programme notes and groove weight — and `synth`, which had been staging in the fallback house in plain concert dress under *"a new one, and nobody has decided about it yet"*, finally has a room.
- **A polysynth was staged in 1952.** Every pre-electronic synth performer came from the pad layer and nowhere else; 1952 is a Carnatic recital whose pad is the tanpura. `rigPoolFor` now returns an empty pool rather than its nearest rig, which is the only answer that cannot be wrong, and eight eras across six genres lost electronic pads they should never have named — finnfolk had a *warm synth pad in 1780*.
- **A cable ran through a player.** The sampling comment reasoned "a sample every 8 cm against a 12 cm clearance", true of the line handed to `settle` and false of everything it gave back — eviction moves points radially and the last rounds do not smooth, leaving a mean segment of 13.5 cm and a longest of 77. No clearance was widened; over-long segments are subdivided and re-settled.
- **A hand was asked for nineteen semitones.** Three causes, one symptom: synth was mis-cutting (fixed by choosing the cut by fewest grabs rather than widest gap), electric-piano had chords spanning 38 semitones that *no* cut can split, and the accordion — 146 of the 152 — never reached the cut branch at all, because `ACCORDION_BUTTON_TOP` is a physical wall with no second hand behind it. The chord **rolls** now, a sixteenth early, because a player is ready before the beat and never after: 0.11% of key notes, nothing dropped. The `HandSpec` was not narrowed, on the grounds that a tenth on an accordion is good music.
- **A seam `elide` could empty the section it arrived at.** On a style sparse enough that the arriving section's only attack was its downbeat, moving it an eighth early moved every onset into the section before. `activeLayers: []` — the IR saying the band left.
- **The fourth constraint casting solves for was never checked.** `DEFAULT_CAMERA` had been exported since it was written, under a comment saying it existed *so the verifier could assert against the same camera the stager staged for*, and nothing ever did. The predicate is `stacked`/`seenAs` now and the check is green at 0.000% of 5948 occluding pairs — floor-seating improved it, from 20 of 13328 to 8 of 13446, because a player who is neither tall nor at the back is not an obstacle.

## 8. What is actually next

**The genre set is complete.** Nineteen genres, 389 styles, 72 eras. The last two — `dnb` and `house` — were written against a §1 with nothing left in it, and they used what had been built for them immediately: house is `breakdown`'s author at 187–200 of 200 placement across eleven styles, and dnb took it on five more.

Both **declined the tempo ramp for the same reason**, which is the most useful thing either of them reported: *a record whose tempo moves cannot be beatmatched*. dnb names it on exactly one style, `breakcore`, at one draw in four. That is a better answer than the feature, and it means the build — a ramp arriving at a drop, the one shape §6 left expressible rather than built — is wanted by neither of the two genres it was expected to serve. It should be built when something actually asks for it.

**The drop has twelve authors**: reggae `dub` and funk `minneapolis`, which reported the gap; ten more across house and dnb. The refusals are in §7 and are the more useful list — nine styles were measured, found feasible, and turned down on musical grounds.

### What is left

Nothing blocks a genre now. What remains, in the order it would repay attention:

**The top four entries on this list and both staging leftovers are gone** — §2.2, §3.16 and the two rooms were the adoption wave in §7, §1.1 closed before it, §3.15 closed after, and §3.17 closed after that. **§3.1 and §3.2 closed after that**, taking two more multi-genre entries with them — two reporters and three. **And §3.3 closed after that, which was thought to be the last of them**: no entry below was reported by more than one genre. **That was wrong, and §3.5 is the counter-example** — funk filed it and house's `speedgarage` hit the same wall from the other side, and this document never counted the second because house wrote the complaint into a paragraph about the bass glide rather than into a filter table. This document counts reporters by where a complaint was *filed*, and the wobble was filed under the wrong heading. It has closed too. Every remaining one has a single author, and none of them removes a technique. That is a different state from the one this section has described at every previous writing, where the head of the list was always something a genre could not play.

**§3.3 is also the first entry to close by having half of it refused on the record.** Arabic took the mechanism and 186 of 200 songs now change maqam mid-piece; indian read it, measured it, and declined, because the gesture it wanted lives inside a phrase and this one fires at a section boundary. Arabic's own `taqsim` declined on the same argument, reached independently. A closure that names where the mechanism *stops* is worth more than one that reports only where it fires, and this list should ask for that shape more often — the note-level figure here (2,165 of 81,975 notes in a departing section actually sound the degree that moved) is the kind of number that tells the next author whether a feature is doing what its section count claims.

1. **§3.19 is closed, and it was the documentation gap** — the fourth in this stretch, after §1.3, §2.3 and §3.20. 12/8 is `beatsPerBar: 6` with `groups: [6, 6, 6, 6]`, `classical` had been shipping two styles in it for two days before `pop/styles.ts` and `rnb/styles.ts` were written, and nothing was built. `doowop` and `deepsoul` are converted; `girlgroup` is measured and deliberately left in its shuffle. **What the entry actually wanted and could not have is different from what it asked for**: one `Style`, two metres. See §3.19 for the numbers, including the one that made `swing` look equivalent for so long — 1.4 ms on the second eighth, and 67.6 ms on the middle triplet nobody was measuring.
2. **§3.20 is withdrawn** — and it is the third entry in one pass to have been describing code that no longer existed, after §1.3 and §2.3. There *is* an object for a person holding a microphone: `ARCHETYPES.singer`, a mic, a stand and a cable, item 32 on the bench. The singer *is* downstage of the keyboard row, measured at exactly `lip − 0.950 m` and a median `|x|` of `0.000` in 131 of 131 sung numbers, with nobody in front of them or level with them in 0 of 125. Nothing was built, because there was nothing to build. **Three stale entries in one pass is the finding.** This document is a list of things the engine cannot say, and it has no mechanism at all for noticing when one of them becomes sayable — every closure so far has depended on the agent who did the work also remembering to come here. A pass that *re-measures the open entries* rather than acting on them would have caught all three, and is probably worth more than the next entry on this list.
3. **§1.3 is closed, and so is the sweep behind it** — it closed by measurement rather than by mechanism. The wall is 24 semitones, not the twelfth six genres wrote around; nothing in the catalogue folds; the fix was a check that names the number. Told the number, **three genres took a note back** — metal's `fifths` at 17, reggae's `steppers-octave` at 17, funk's `octave-drop` at 14 — and **three re-read their tables and moved nothing**, because a sub, an 808 and a 303 line are each one register wide and the octave they all write is twelve semitones from either end. The funk figures that copied the ♭7-above spelling were checked one at a time and kept — **thirteen of them, not the eleven this document counted**, because it had counted the ones with a comment: nine sit in a line that also touches the octave, four never touch it. **The lesson is the ratio.** Half the genres that reported this entry were not being held back by it at all — they were being held back by not knowing, and the cost of the silence was a comment in each of them telling the next author a number that was wrong. Notably the glide adoption never made it worse, because `generateBass` folds a destination into the same span reduce and every author kept their destinations inside tones the figure already struck.

**`DrumTrack.voiceEffects` had a home and no door — closed, see §6.** It was the clearest §7 case in the project: read by `render/strudel.ts`, written by nothing, **0 of 380 songs across all nineteen genres**, while four files in two genres cited it as the field that exists for the gated snare. `voiceEffects` is now on `Genre`, `EraProfile` and `Style`, and rock's `arena` is the one table that writes it. **What makes it a closure rather than another unused field is that the adoption deleted its own workaround** — `arena` had spelled the gate as a whole-kit reverb with the ceiling pulled down, and that number is gone rather than sitting next to the real one. The two remaining citations, both in pop, are left for pop's author; the door is open and the argument for walking through it is theirs to make with their own numbers.

**A second `duckSource` is the one field the sidechain left expressible and unbuilt.** The trigger is the kick by name, on `Style.breakCarrier`'s reasoning — a rule that *searches* for its trigger gives the same style a kick-keyed duck in one song and a clap-keyed one in the next. Every one of the seven adopting styles keys off the kick and so does every record any of them describes, so the field would be generality nothing uses, which is what §7 says is worse than an absence. It should be built when somebody asks with a figure attached.

**§3.15 closed and left dnb unadopted on purpose**, which is the one live thread out of this pass. That genre reported the gap in the sharpest words — *no stutter, no retrigger, no 32nd roll* — and its idiom wants the mechanism somewhere hiphop's does not: a dnb stutter is a gesture on the *snare* and often across a whole beat rather than inside one sixteenth, which is four rolled strokes in a row here and should be written by somebody who has listened to the records rather than inferred them from trap's. The field is proven and the adoption is a table edit; what it needs is an author, not a mechanism.

**Two mechanical gaps named by the adoption wave**, both small and both with a measurement attached rather than a guess. Indian's dhrupad and vilambit are on ektāl, whose sixes give six onsets where `bandHeads` defines a shot as two to four — a `shots` table per style would fix it, and that author declined to invent a chautāl tihai they had not measured. And `BassHit.glide` cannot begin its travel *late*, which `NoteBend` argues is forced by superdough's envelope having no delay stage; dnb showed the gesture is sayable in two notes, so this wants a second field only if somebody asks with a number attached.

**`breakdown` still has no honest author**, and the measurement explaining why is worth keeping. Its witness is `pad` and it removes `drums` and `bass`, so it needs a dance record with a wash, on a form long enough for three four-bar phrases. The catalogue's dance records are all built on eight-bar sections: **0 of 30 at four bars across all 24 pop styles**. `dancepop` reaches 200/200 at `dropBars: 2`, but two bars of kit-and-bass-gone at 124–132 BPM is under four seconds, and that gap is *already* authored — pop weights the `drop` fill at 4 and its index says "from 1982 onward the commonest thing that happens in the bar before a chorus is that everything stops." Adopting it there would say the same thing twice in two units. The styles that *do* place a four-bar `breakdown` cleanly are latin, classical, synth, indian, arabic and iskelmä, and none of them is a dance record. So it waits for the first house style, which is what it was shaped for.

**Nothing is a blocker any more, and that is a change of state rather than a milestone.** This section used to end by naming tempo ramps as the largest remaining item by blast radius; §1.1 closed, and §8's own top three closed after it, the last of them being §3.17. The work that repays attention now is not *unblocking* a genre — it is the sort of thing §7's second wave turned out to be, where the value came less from the fields than from three authors reading source and finding their brief was wrong. **The next useful pass is probably a sweep for stale comments rather than a mechanism**, because this document's own record is that a fixed limitation leaves its apology behind in the code, and every one of those is a future author writing a compromise nobody needs.

**The sidechain wave is the evidence for that sentence rather than a counter-example to it.** Closing §3.17 meant rewriting five apologies in two genres, and three of them were wrong about something other than the limitation they were apologising for: pop's `sidechain` era cited a section of `pop/index.ts` that has never existed, house's index made a claim about its own comp tables that is false of the two heaviest figures in the two styles it named, and four files across pop and rock cite `DrumTrack.voiceEffects` as a solved problem in a field no author can write to. None of those was found by looking for stale comments. All three were found by an author trying to delete one.

**§3.1 and §3.2 closed together, and between them they make the same point a third and a fourth time.** Classical's title generator refused the key on the grounds that it would force a spelling decision into a type — and `keyLabel` was being called for every song in the catalogue three lines below the call that refused it, and had been since before the genre existed. Arabic's maqam was a pure function of two numbers the context did not carry, and a lookup that asked only the genre would have mislabelled every taqsim. Neither of those was a limitation; both were **an author correctly reporting what they could not reach and nobody afterwards re-reading whether it had become reachable**, which is §8's own item 2 restated by two more files. The measurement that settles both is cheap and neither entry had it: generate the same seed twice with one field flipped, and count what actually changed rather than what looks like it should have.

**One methodological finding worth more than either entry.** The obvious probe for §3.1 — count notes on the final downbeat — gives numbers of the right size and attributes them to the wrong mechanism, because most of those notes were already there. It was the probe this pass started with and it survived a first round of writing before the A/B caught it. **A number that looks plausible against a prediction is the hardest kind to check**, and the only defence is to measure the difference rather than the state.
